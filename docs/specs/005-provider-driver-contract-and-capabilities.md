> **Amendment (2026-08-18, provider-account and usage-limit signal — amends the previously-`approved` spec; the header flips to `review` under the audit runbook's spec-amendment rule and is restored `approved` by the paired Plan-005 targeted readiness-audit delta riding this same diff, the in-swap flip-and-restore shape the 2026-08-17 amendment cohort establishes).** Two additive legs, both driver-contract concerns this spec had left unaddressed because they sat behind a Non-Goal that was drawn too wide. (1) **The typed provider usage-limit signal.** A provider refusing a turn because the account's plan allowance is spent is not a failure of the run, a fault of the driver, or a recovery condition — it is a pacing fact with a reset boundary. It becomes a **sibling axis beside `RecoveryCondition`**, not a member of it: a closed-set cause with an optional reset boundary carrying its own provenance (§Fallback Behavior). Recognition is **typed-only** — a driver emits the signal from a structured provider event it can name, never by matching message prose or exit codes — because a false positive parks a healthy run and a false negative burns the allowance silently. An unrecognized shape yields **no signal**, which the pacing consumer treats as absence rather than as absence-of-limit. (2) **Provider-account identity at spawn.** Session-creation and resume parameters gain an **additive-optional** account identifier, and the driver receives the credential environment the daemon constructs for that account rather than reading ambient provider credentials (§Interfaces And Contracts). The account plane itself — registration, defaults, credential homes, validation, concurrency posture — is owned by [Spec-029](029-provider-accounts-and-credential-homes.md); this spec owns only the driver-facing seam. Both legs are strictly additive to the shipped Phase-1/Phase-2 contract surface: no existing field changes type, no required field is added, and a caller that supplies neither behaves exactly as before.

> **Amendment (2026-08-28, usage-delta accounting and child-thread frame routing — amends the previously-`approved` spec; the header flips to `review` under the audit runbook's spec-amendment rule and is restored `approved` by the paired Plan-005 targeted readiness-audit delta riding this same diff, the in-swap flip-and-restore shape the 2026-08-17 amendment cohort establishes).** Two legs, both closing hazards this spec had never named because it specified what a driver emits and never what its numbers **mean** or whose stream they arrived on. **(1) Provider usage counters are cumulative and this spec required nothing about it.** Both pinned surfaces report a running total for the provider session rather than a figure for the turn that just finished, and both report a cached-input figure that is a **component of** their input figure rather than a sibling of it (the frame shapes Verified against the vendor's own generated schema, the reset-boundary behavior and magnitudes Derived from a third-party reference-implementation audit — see §References). A normalizer that forwards each reading as a per-turn figure re-counts every earlier turn on every later one — measured **22×** overstatement of session spend on a long thread — and one that forwards the input figure beside the cached figure double-counts every cached token. Because [Spec-016 §Session Cost Receipt](016-multi-agent-channels-and-orchestration.md#session-cost-receipt) folds exactly these rows into the committed-spend figure this product bills and budgets against, the defect lands on money rather than on a display. §Required Behavior gains the **per-turn delta rule** (cumulative in, delta out; one base per provider **thread** and axis, advanced in stream order as each reading is consumed — never a base snapshot copied at turn dispatch; each interval attributed to the turn the metered frame itself **names**; base establishment two-armed — zero for a fresh provider session, replay-seeded included, and the daemon's own prior-emitted cumulative sum for a provider-native resume; no re-base at a compaction; a decrease is a falsified declaration floored at zero with a diagnostic, never negative spend; a wire-declared per-turn figure corroborates the derived interval and never substitutes for it) and the **non-overlapping axis rule** (the normalized input axis is uncached input, with any cached-read component subtracted at the boundary exactly where the breakdown's own arithmetic identity confirms the containment). **(2) A provider connection carries more than one thread and this spec assumed one.** Both pinned providers multiplex child-thread traffic — subagent, review, and compaction threads the provider spawns for itself — over the connection that carries the session's own thread. Projecting every received frame writes a child's words into the parent's transcript as though the parent produced them, and either double-counts the child's spend or loses it. §Required Behavior gains the **child-frame routing rule**: classify every inbound frame by its family against the pinned stream-surface census — thread-scoped families route by explicit thread identity while registered connection- and account-scoped families route without one — register the session's own thread at establishment and admit child identities from the provider's parent-linked announcements, hold a present-but-unregistered identity in a bounded pending-registration buffer released on registration, quarantine-and-report an absent or unrecognized identity rather than projecting it (fail closed), carve **both** usage and child-originated interactive requests out **before** suppression — so no token is dropped and no child tool call hangs, the requests riding the same approval pipeline as the parent's per [Spec-016 §Provider-Native Subagents](016-multi-agent-channels-and-orchestration.md#provider-native-subagents) — and surface child lifecycle only through the already-registered `subagent.*` kinds. **Nothing is minted and no census moves.** The deltas are internal emission semantics over the already-registered [Spec-006 §Usage Telemetry](006-session-event-taxonomy-and-audit-log.md#usage-telemetry-usage_telemetry) payload; child spend rides that payload's existing `subagentId` carrier and the run scope it already defines for its absence; `subagent.started` / `subagent.completed` are already registered event types. One limit is recorded rather than papered over: that payload carries **no per-cache-axis member**, so the cache-read/cache-write split is retained on the driver's diagnostic band and named as an ordinary Spec-006 growth for whichever consumer first needs it — minting it here would mint a member ahead of its reader, which §Capability discovery rejects for capability flags on reasoning that applies unchanged to payload members. **The 2026-08-28 review fold (Codex round 1, eight accepted findings) rides this same swap**: the delta base is re-specified per provider thread with named-turn attribution (the per-turn-keyed base it replaces double-counted interleaved turns), the establishment arms are split fresh-zero versus resume-at-prior-emitted-sum (the unconditional zero-meter first reading it replaces dropped a fresh session's first billed turn), the routing rule is family-scoped with a parent-linked registration path, a bounded pending-registration hold, and a child-request carve-through, the evidence is re-grounded on the vendor's own generated schema (see §References), and the cross-document registration lands: [Spec-006 §Usage Telemetry](006-session-event-taxonomy-and-audit-log.md#usage-telemetry-usage_telemetry) re-describes `usage.token_count` as a per-turn figure whose scope totals fold by summation (Spec-006 flip-and-restored `approved` in the same swap; description-only, its taxonomy census unmoved at 159), and Plan-016's CP-016-9 registers the budget accountant's sum-fold consumption of those rows on a recorded no-flip.

## Purpose

Define the normalized driver boundary between the core runtime and provider-specific execution transports.

## Scope

This spec covers required driver operations, capability advertisement, normalized event shapes, and runtime binding persistence.

## Non-Goals

- UI behavior for every capability
- Provider-specific prompt tuning
- Provider commercial or billing concerns — **narrowed 2026-08-18.** Pricing, plan selection, invoicing, and what a provider charges remain out of scope. Two adjacent facts are **in** scope, because both are driver-contract surfaces rather than commercial ones: the typed provider usage-limit signal (§Fallback Behavior), which is a pacing fact the runtime must act on rather than a billing figure, and the provider-account identity a run is spawned against (§Interfaces And Contracts), which selects a credential environment. Neither carries a price, a rate, or an amount owed. What is spent and what it cost belong to `Spec-016 §Session Cost Receipt`; which account paid and under which billing mode belong to `Spec-029 §Billing mode`.

## Required Behavior

- Every provider integration must implement a normalized driver contract.
- Provider drivers must execute within a participant-owned or operator-owned runtime node governed by a local daemon. V1 must not depend on a shared hosted driver service as the execution authority.
- The driver contract must support create session, resume session, start run, interrupt run, `applyIntervention`, respond to interactive request, close session, and enumerate models or modes or both where available.
- The `applyIntervention` operation is a generic dispatcher that routes intervention requests (steer, interrupt, cancel) to the appropriate driver-specific handler based on the intervention type and the driver's declared capabilities. `cancel` maps to the `interrupted` terminal state in the run state machine — it is a user-initiated interruption. Drivers that do not support a given intervention type must return a `degraded` result, allowing the orchestration layer to fall back (e.g., steer degrades to queue + interrupt for providers without native steer support). The driver-level result vocabulary is exactly `applied | degraded`; `rejected` and `expired` are orchestration-layer verdicts rendered by the daemon's authorization and version-guard checks around driver dispatch and are never returned by a driver — the normative mapping onto the six-state intervention lifecycle lives in [Queue And Intervention Model §Driver Result To Lifecycle Mapping](../domain/queue-and-intervention-model.md#driver-result-to-lifecycle-mapping).
- The contract additionally defines capability-gated parity operations — `rollbackTo` (conversation rollback to a normalized session position on the run's live provider binding; gated on the `rollback` flag), `setSessionGoal` / `clearSessionGoal` (session-goal injection; gated on `session_goals`), and `replayTranscript` (canonical-transcript reconstitution into a fresh provider session, 2026-08-26; gated on `transcript_replay`) — plus a zero-turn `probeAuth` required of every driver: provider authentication state must be knowable without spending a billed turn. Dispatch of a capability-gated parity operation follows the same static/dynamic split as interventions ([Queue And Intervention Model](../domain/queue-and-intervention-model.md)): a call against a flag the driver does not declare is refused statically with `driver.capability_unsupported` before driver dispatch — with exactly one exception, `replayTranscript`, whose undeclared arm has a named daemon-side fallback and therefore settles `degraded` on the memo projection rather than refusing (§The memo projection is the floor; [ADR-029 §Enumerated Gates And Fallbacks](../decisions/029-canonical-transcript-is-authoritative.md#enumerated-gates-and-fallbacks)); `rollbackTo` and the session-goal pair have no documented orchestration fallback and refuse — while `degraded` is the dynamic outcome of an invoked driver that could not deliver the operation natively and reports the fallback it took — never an opaque failure.
- Every intervention request carries a mandatory requester-generated `clientIdempotencyKey` (UUID) — generated by the participant client, or by the daemon’s origination path for system interventions (orchestration budget / idle / moderation interrupts per ADR-011), which reuses its synthesized key across its own retry loop. The daemon applies replay-or-conflict semantics — an identical retry returns the originally recorded outcome without re-dispatching the driver; reuse of a key with a differing payload is rejected as `intervention.idempotency_conflict` — following the converging idempotency-key practice ([Stripe idempotent requests](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests); the expired IETF draft [draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header](https://datatracker.ietf.org/doc/draft-ietf-httpapi-idempotency-key-header/)). The key rides through `ApplyInterventionParams` so drivers can propagate it to provider-remote invocations that honor dedupe keys (the `compensable` pattern, §Tool Metadata).
- Every `getCapabilities` report must include `cliVersion` — a `DriverCliVersionReport` carrying the verbatim provider-reported version string (`raw`) and its parsed semantic version (`semver`). The daemon enforces a per-driver minimum-version floor mechanically at attach and refresh (semantic-version comparison against the configured floor; V1 floors: Claude Code `2.1.234`, codex-cli `0.141.0` — set by this spec, raised for Claude Code on 2026-08-26, and mirrored by the nightly compatibility check's `floor` legs). An unparseable version report fails closed: the report cannot be constructed (`semver` is required, so unparsed-but-attached state is unrepresentable), the driver refuses attach, and the failure surfaces as `driver.cli_version_unparseable`. A version that parses cleanly but sits below the configured floor likewise refuses attach/refresh fail-closed, surfacing as `driver.cli_version_below_floor` (distinct from `version.floor_exceeded`, which governs client/event-envelope contract floors, not provider CLI installs).
- **The floor is this spec's to set; the pin is the reference family's to record (2026-08-26).** They answer different questions — the floor is the oldest build the driver accepts, the pin is the newest build whose shapes were verified — and they move independently, so a re-pin records what was measured without raising what is supported. A governing document therefore **states a floor and cites a pin**: the floors above are values this spec sets, and every version number a spec or plan names for a _measured_ provider surface is a citation of [`claude.md`](../reference/provider-wire/claude.md) / [`codex.md`](../reference/provider-wire/codex.md) rather than a copy of one, because a copied pin is a value the next re-pin cannot reach. The Claude Code floor moved `2.1.198` → `2.1.234` in this amendment; codex-cli's is unchanged.
- **A build at or above the floor is admitted, and a build above the pin is not a refusal condition (2026-08-26).** The floor comparison is the whole of the version gate: at or above it the driver attaches, whether or not the build is one this corpus has measured. Newer-than-measured is the **expected** state rather than an exception — both vendors publish faster than any authoring pass can re-verify — so a driver that refused an untested build would refuse most of its own installed base within weeks. What an untested build changes is not admission but confidence: its capabilities are decided by the per-capability rule below rather than inherited from a table, and a surface that moved withdraws **that one capability**, never the session. This is the all-or-nothing-free policy the [provider-wire reference](../reference/provider-wire/README.md#versioning-and-pinning-policy) family states; the sentence here is its normative statement, and the two must not diverge.
- **Every optional capability is decided by a declared per-capability detection mechanism, and the report records how each flag was decided (2026-08-26).** A flag declared from a static per-driver table is a claim about a build the table's author measured, not about the build that is running. Each flag on `GetCapabilitiesResult` therefore carries a **detection source** — a closed two-value `static | probed` — and each flag's mechanism is **declared per capability rather than assumed uniform**, because probeability is a property of the capability, not of the build. A probe is **admissible** only when it is all three of: **zero-turn** (it establishes availability without starting a model turn, and so is zero-billed); **non-mutating** (it changes no state the session owns — an operation whose defined effect is to _replace_ a set is not probeable by invoking it with an empty set, because the probe would perform the replacement); and **decisive at the granularity the capability is consumed at** (a capability whose contract depends on a parameter is not decided by the method's presence, so an enumeration that lists the method establishes the method and not the parameter). A flag with no admissible probe is declared `static` **by construction and by declaration** — the mechanism table names the conjunct that fails — and is left to its invocation-time refusal. `static` is therefore a stated property of a capability, never a silent fallback for a probe that was not written. Both providers expose such a channel at the pin (Verified 2026-08-26): the Claude control-request dispatcher answers an unknown subtype with a typed `Unsupported control request subtype: <name>` and a supported one with `success`, on the driver's own streaming-input transport and with no user message sent; the Codex app-server answers an unknown method with `-32600` **enumerating the accepted method set**, so one malformed request enumerates the surface. A probe is admissible only alongside a **negative control** — a deliberately unsupported name that must still be refused — because a probe that silently stopped probing reports every capability available. Failure is per capability: a probe that refuses withdraws exactly the flag it probed. Nothing is minted for this: an invocation against a withdrawn capability refuses as the already-registered `driver.capability_unsupported`, and a flag that moves `true` → `false` between two snapshots surfaces on the existing change-detected `runtime_node.capability_updated` emission, which already carries previous and new capability snapshots.
- **Registry membership is neither necessary nor sufficient for availability (2026-08-26).** The reference family already records the sufficiency half — a subtype present in a build's own registry can still be refused at call time. The **necessity** half was measured on 2026-08-26 and is the sharper one: the `mcp_set_servers` control request answers `{ "subtype": "success", … }` at `2.1.234`, `2.1.245`, and `2.1.246`, while appearing in **none** of those builds' censused subtype registries. A census is therefore a lower bound on availability exactly as a registry is an upper bound on it, and neither direction may decide a capability. What decides it is the flag's declared mechanism: an admissible probe's own classified answer where one exists, and the matrix reading the mechanism table declares otherwise. **The measurement above is authoring-time evidence, not a runtime mechanism** — it was taken out of band against a scratch session, and it is precisely the call the non-mutating conjunct forbids the driver to issue against a live one, which is also why `mcp_set_servers` is **not** one of the seventeen capability flags: it is a control-request subtype, no flag is decided by issuing it, and its runtime availability is Plan-028's own reconcile answer rather than anything this driver reports.
- **The version a driver reports is the version that spawned (2026-08-26).** The driver resolves the executable, spawns it, and reads the version **in-band from the running process** — Claude's `get_binary_version` control request returns that build's own `version` and `buildTime`; Codex's `initialize` result returns a `userAgent` **containing** the running `codex-cli` version — both zero-turn. The two readings are not symmetric, and the spec says so rather than papering over it: the Claude channel is structured (`{ version, buildTime }`), while the Codex channel is a **composite** string that also carries the caller's own client name and version, so a driver reading it MUST extract rather than adopt. Codex's structured alternative is not available to V1 — `server/diagnostics` is gated on the `experimentalApi` capability and, granted it, returns process gauges carrying no version at all (Verified 2026-08-26 at `0.149.1`) — so the composite `userAgent` is the only non-experimental in-band channel, and the extraction rule is a normative obligation on the driver rather than an implementation detail. A launcher symlink or wrapper is not an acceptable source: at this pin `claude --version` names the launcher's current build, which is measurably not the build a path-addressed spawn runs, and that divergence is how the reference file's own census target and `claude --version` came apart. One reading serves both consumers — it is what the floor comparison compares and what the run's `runtime_bindings` row records (`cli_version_raw` / `cli_version_semver`, with the resolved executable path on the daemon-owned `spawn_config` carrier) — so the recorded version can never describe a different install from the one that ran, and a replay reads what actually ran. No column is minted; both carriers already ship.
- **Provider auto-update is disabled in every driver-spawned child (2026-08-26).** The daemon owns the spawn environment for every provider process, and every such environment sets that provider's documented auto-update opt-out — for Claude Code, `DISABLE_AUTOUPDATER=1` and `DISABLE_UPDATES=1` ([Claude wire reference](../reference/provider-wire/claude.md), presence-style gates). A build that replaces itself mid-session invalidates two things at once: the version recorded on the run's binding stops describing the process, and the capability snapshot the run was admitted against was probed from a binary that is no longer executing. Suppression is a correctness obligation, not hygiene. Where a provider documents no opt-out, the driver reaches the same guarantee by resolving and spawning an **exact build path** rather than a floating launcher; a driver that can do neither declares no `probed` flags, because it cannot promise the probed build is the running one.
- **Exactly one member of a mutually-exclusive provider posture pair is realized (2026-08-26).** Codex `turn/start` carries both a legacy `sandboxPolicy` and a preferred `permissions` profile selection, and the vendor's generated schema states they cannot be combined. Two measurements (Verified 2026-08-26 at `codex-cli 0.149.1`) make this a driver-boundary rule rather than a caller convention. First, which member is even sendable is a property of the negotiated connection: on a **default** connection `permissions` is refused **by name** — `-32600 turn/start.permissions requires experimentalApi capability` — while `sandboxPolicy` deserializes. Second, on an `experimentalApi` connection the two sent **together** are **not** refused at request validation, so the documented exclusivity is unenforced at the wire and a driver sending both would get an unspecified precedence rather than an error. The driver therefore realizes the daemon-derived posture through **exactly one** member and treats the un-realized member as a posture-affecting field the daemon did not derive — refused rather than passed through ([Spec-012 §Required Behavior](012-approvals-permissions-and-trust-boundaries.md#required-behavior) owns the daemon-side half). V1 realizes the **non-experimental** member, `sandboxPolicy`: the Codex driver opts into `experimentalApi` for the interactive-request leg, so `permissions` is reachable on its connection, but a posture write must not rest on a field the vendor may reshape behind an experimental gate — the read-tolerant / write-gated split, and a different axis from the modern-surface preference that governs continuity verbs. `permissions` leaving the experimental gate, observable as its arrival in the default generation, re-decides this by ordinary amendment. The removed `permissionProfile` field is not a third option: it refuses `-32602` on both connection kinds at this pin.
- Every driver must implement `probeAuth` — a zero-turn authentication probe returning `DriverAuthProbeResult` (`authenticated | unauthenticated | indeterminate`). `indeterminate` (probe surface unavailable or unparseable) is treated as not authenticated for admission — fail closed — while remaining distinguishable so operators can separate probe health from credential state. Run admission against a driver that does not probe `authenticated` is refused as `driver.not_authenticated` before any turn is spent.
- The Codex driver must pin `approvalsReviewer: "user"` at session spawn so every approval routes through the daemon's approval pipeline ([Spec-012](012-approvals-permissions-and-trust-boundaries.md)); provider-side guardian / auto-review auto-adjudication is disabled by contract. The generated protocol's own default is already `user`, so the pin is defense-in-depth against a config/profile override — and guardian / auto-approval notifications normalize as observability rows, never as approval decisions.
- Spawn/turn parameters carry the parity surfaces: a session callback-tool registry (`SessionCallbackTool[]` — the daemon exposes a curated tool set into every run; Codex leg `dynamicTools` + the `item/tool/call` server request, Claude leg a daemon-hosted ephemeral MCP server via `--mcp-config`; every callback invocation flows through the daemon's approval pipeline per [Spec-012](012-approvals-permissions-and-trust-boundaries.md) and lands as ordinary `tool_activity` rows per [Spec-006](006-session-event-taxonomy-and-audit-log.md)), a `subagentPolicy { enabled, maxDepth, maxConcurrent, definitions[] }` pass-through under the single-supervisor invariant (the daemon is the only cross-session supervisor; orchestration semantics land in Spec-016 via campaign B6), and the run's `executionPosture` (shape owned by this spec and typed in [API Payload Contracts](../architecture/contracts/api-payload-contracts.md); authorization and policy semantics in [Spec-012 §Required Behavior](012-approvals-permissions-and-trust-boundaries.md#required-behavior), campaign B20).
- Drivers must emit normalized runtime events rather than leaking provider-native event types into the session engine.
- Drivers must declare capability flags for at least `resume`, `steer`, `interactive_requests`, `mcp`, `tool_calls`, `reasoning_stream`, `model_mutation`, `structured_output`, `rollback`, `session_goals`, `callback_tools`, `subagents`, `transcript_replay`, `cost_cap`, `context_compaction`, `provider_commands`, and `output_speed` (the last three added 2026-08-29). A flag declares that the driver delivers the capability through its own mechanism at the driver boundary — natively or driver-emulated; **how** it delivers is recorded as a mechanism grade on the parity capability matrix (a capability delivered by the orchestration layer above the driver, like steer-for-Claude, keeps its driver flag `false`). The `pause` flag is intentionally excluded — no current provider implements native pause. Pause is an orchestration-layer construct (interrupt run, persist state, queue resume) that does not require driver support.
- Drivers must persist provider-owned resume handles separately from canonical session and run ids.
- The runtime must treat undeclared capabilities as unsupported.
- Drivers must declare a per-tool `idempotency_class ∈ {idempotent, compensable, manual_reconcile_only}` alongside their tool list. This metadata controls crash-recovery behavior for in-flight side-effecting tool calls; see [Spec-015 § Idempotency Protocol](015-persistence-recovery-and-replay.md#idempotency-protocol) for recovery semantics.
- **Provider-bound participant text reaches the model as prose, or the turn fails visibly (2026-08-25).** Text a participant authored — a run's opening content, a steer directive's content, and admitted queue content re-entering through run start — is data _for the model_, never an instruction to the provider's own client. Where a provider's programmatic input surface also parses client-side commands, a message whose first word is command-shaped is consumed by that parser and the participant's words never reach the model; the observed failure is silent, returning a zero-turn success rather than an error (Claude Code `2.1.245`, verified 2026-08-25). Every driver must therefore route provider-bound text through a single **outbound frame boundary**, and a driver whose provider command-parses that surface must neutralize command-shaped participant text there so the provider parses it as prose. The boundary and the tripwire below are **universal**; the transform itself is **grade-scoped** to the drivers whose mechanism grade requires it (§Parity Capability Mechanism Grades). A driver graded `native` on that row passes the author's bytes through unchanged — prepending a sentinel a provider does not need would alter text it would otherwise have delivered exactly. Because the grade is the behavioral input here rather than a bare record, **that row states the grade governing today, never the one a provider is expected to earn**: a `native` claim resting on any frontend other than the transport the driver actually drives is recorded `emulated` until a probe of that transport confirms it, and the confirming re-grade is an ordinary amendment. The default is therefore the transform and not its absence, because a spare newline on a prompt costs nothing and a skipped one costs the whole message. And the grade is a claim the tripwire keeps honest rather than one that is trusted: a `native` driver whose provider starts command-dispatching after an update trips exactly as an `emulated` one does, and the remedy is re-grading it, not discovering the loss from a user. **Command-shaped** is one predicate with one implementation shared by every driver, so two drivers can never classify the same message differently: the frame's first non-whitespace byte is `/` (`0x2F`), where **whitespace is exactly the six ASCII bytes** `0x09` (tab), `0x0A` (line feed), `0x0B` (vertical tab), `0x0C` (form feed), `0x0D` (carriage return), and `0x20` (space) — skipped from the frame's first byte until the first byte that is not one of them, which decides. Enumerating that set is load-bearing rather than pedantic: _whitespace_ is the one term in this predicate two drivers could read differently, and a byte-level rule may not appeal to a Unicode property without the decoding this rule forbids. A frame led by a non-ASCII whitespace character — a UTF-8 no-break space, say — is therefore **not** command-shaped here, and that residual is left to the tripwire deliberately rather than closed by guessing at a wider set: should a provider ever be observed trimming such bytes before its own command parse, the trip is loud, the turn fails visibly, and the remedy is widening this enumeration by amendment against an observation — where widening it today would trade a named residual for an unnamed one. It is deliberately that coarse. It is byte-level and locale-independent — it never decodes, normalizes, or case-folds — and it consults no list of known command names, because an unknown name is precisely the case that fails silently. It draws no distinction a provider might not draw: `/foo`, `/foo:bar` (the plugin-qualified command form), and the path-like `/etc/hosts` are all command-shaped, because the only cost of neutralizing prose is one invisible byte on the wire while the cost of missing a command is the whole message. A slash anywhere but the first non-whitespace position is not command-shaped. Neutralization is **transport-only**: it changes only the bytes on the provider wire, never the canonical text this repo persists, events, replays, rewinds to, or shows a participant, so an audit row and a rollback target carry exactly what the author typed. This is a **delivery-correctness** rule, not the provider-specific prompt tuning §Non-Goals excludes: the transform changes no word the author wrote, and it applies only where the alternative is the message not arriving at all. The V1 sentinel is a single leading newline — the smallest edit that defeats the parse. Should a provider build ever parse through it, the escalation ladder is ordered by decreasing invisibility and increasing honesty: leading newline → leading space → structural repositioning of the command-shaped word → a visible, documented prefix → a typed refusal. A driver never silently drops the message, and a driver that exhausts the ladder grades this capability `absent` and refuses command-shaped participant text loudly rather than delivering it mangled. Reaching a provider's own command surface _deliberately_ is a separate concern this spec does not open: the composer-level escape is [Spec-017](017-workflow-authoring-and-execution.md)'s C-18, which activates on provider-bound composer paths once this neutralization ships.
- **Neutralization is decided by a closed frame origin, and an unknown origin neutralizes (2026-08-25).** Every outbound provider frame declares exactly one origin — `participant_text` (neutralized), `driver_command` (a command the _driver itself_ composed from a typed daemon intent, routed verbatim and exempt from the tripwire below so the provider's own command surface stays reachable), or `system_narration` (daemon-composed prose, neutralized). An absent or unrecognized origin **neutralizes**: a frame nobody classified is far likelier to be participant text than a driver command, and neutralizing a command wrongly costs a visible no-op where skipping neutralization wrongly costs a silently swallowed turn. The discriminator is deliberately **not** a capability flag — an undeclared flag resolves through this section's undeclared-is-unsupported rule to "not neutralized", which is the fail-open polarity. **V1 emits exactly one `driver_command` frame, re-derived 2026-08-29 rather than restated.** The 2026-08-25 text this replaces read "V1 emits no `driver_command` frame: **no** capability this spec grades `emulated` for Claude is delivered through the provider's own command surface", and predicted its own falsification — the arm existed "so that a later capability leg realized through a provider's own command surface has a declared route instead of an exemption cut into the neutralizer after the fact". That leg has arrived, and it is not the predicted one: **participant-triggered context compaction** on the Claude leg, which §Participant-triggered context compaction grades `emulated` through the provider's own `/compact`. The count is **exactly one**, and it is re-derived against the two nearest candidates rather than asserted: `model_mutation`, the predicted first, reaches Claude at spawn rather than through a command and remains a non-producer; and the output-speed axis, whose provider does publish an interactive command for the mode, is bound to its **measured** spawn-time settings mechanism instead, so V1 dispatches no command for it either (§The output-speed axis). Re-derivation rather than restatement is the point of stating the predicate as a count: the audit is cheap and the claim stays checkable, where an unenumerated "no capability" would have decayed into a false absolute the first time a grade moved. **The exempt arm is guarded, and its guard is named here because the tripwire does not reach it.** A `driver_command` frame is exempt from the neutralization tripwire — command dispatch being its intended outcome — so the frame that carries a compaction command would otherwise be the one provider-bound frame in V1 with no detector at all. It is guarded on both sides instead, and both guards are obligations rather than advice: **before** dispatch, the command's literal name must be present in the provider's own enumerated command set for that session's binding (§The provider command and skill surface — an enumeration the driver already reads, which is what makes this checkable instead of a hardcoded string the next provider release can retire beneath), and **after** dispatch the operation completes only on the provider's own typed evidence that the command ran, never on the frame settling. A driver that can satisfy neither guard declares `context_compaction: false` and refuses; it does not dispatch a command it cannot confirm. `system_narration` — daemon-composed prose on the same channel — carries exactly **one** V1 producer, audited rather than assumed (this audit was `nil` until 2026-08-26 and the memo projection falsified it, which is why it is re-derived here rather than restated): the **memo projection** of §The memo projection is the floor, which is a turn on the text-input channel composed by the daemon, declares `system_narration` at the outbound frame boundary, and is neutralized like any other daemon-composed prose. The two paths that might look like producers are not: the goal composition this spec grades `emulated` for Claude reaches the provider through system-prompt composition rather than through this boundary, and `respondToRequest` delivers a structured reply correlated to a `requestId` rather than a turn on the text-input channel. It is kept distinct from `participant_text` even though the two neutralize identically, because when the tripwire fires the frame record must say _whose_ words failed to arrive — a policy-composed answer ever delivered as text would otherwise be filed as the participant's.
- **The neutralizer's effect is verified at runtime, not assumed (2026-08-25).** A transform is a claim about a provider parser the daemon neither owns nor pins, and a provider update can change that parser between two runs of the same install. Each outbound frame therefore carries a daemon-minted correlation value, and the driver classifies the provider's response to that frame. **The classification asks for positive evidence of a model turn, never for a recognizable command dispatch.** The set of shapes in which a provider can say _I handled that as my own command_ is open, undocumented at the versions this repo pins, and free to grow with the next release, so a detector that enumerates it is stale on arrival and fails open — silently, in exactly the shape this rule exists to end. The set of shapes that evidence a model turn is closed, small, and already parsed by the normalizer to do its ordinary work. The burden is therefore inverted: a correlated frame that **settles** with no typed event attributing model output to it has tripped, whatever the provider called what it did instead. The classifier reads **typed fields of the provider's own response envelope** and never message prose, which §Pitfalls To Avoid forbids for the same localization and wording-drift reasons it forbids text-matched quota refusals; a response envelope whose shape the driver does not recognize therefore trips rather than passing, and a driver that would rather not trip on an unrecognized shape pins the turn-evidence discriminants for its provider instead of relaxing that polarity. Which typed fields those are is per-provider implementation work the driver records and re-probes, not a set this spec can enumerate: the pinned provider-wire references document no such discriminant at the versions this rule was authored against. One dispatch shape **is** named normatively, as the rule's conformance instance rather than as its definition — the **zero-turn synthetic reply**: a settled correlated frame whose response envelope carries a client-composed reply item, no model-turn attribution for that frame, and no token usage accounted to it. That is the shape observed first-party on Claude Code `2.1.245`, and the recorded transcript of it is [Plan-005](../plans/005-provider-driver-contract-and-capabilities.md) T3.18's **conformance fixture** — every driver's classifier must trip on it. Naming it does not narrow the rule to it: a frame that settles in any other shape without turn evidence trips just the same, so the fixture proves the detector present without becoming the enumeration this rule refuses to build. **A trip fails the run, and there is one landing for both paths.** The daemon emits the run's ordinary `run.failed` terminal carrying `failureCategory: 'provider failure'`, a `recoveryCondition` of `recovery-needed`, and a `providerFailureDetail` in **one fixed daemon-composed form**: the registered `driver.text_neutralization_failed` ([Error Contracts](../architecture/contracts/error-contracts.md)), one space byte (`0x20`), then `origin=` followed by one of `participant_text`, `system_narration`, or `unknown` — so `driver.text_neutralization_failed origin=participant_text` is the entire detail. Writing the form down is what makes _machine-readable_ true rather than claimed: a consumer reads the cause as the substring before the first space and the origin as the substring after `origin=`, and an origin the closed union does not name is recorded as the literal `unknown` rather than echoed, which keeps a string the daemon did not compose out of the audit log. `driver_command` can never appear, those frames being exempt. The form binds **this cause only** — `providerFailureDetail` stays free-form prose for the resume failure that introduced it ([API Payload Contracts](../architecture/contracts/api-payload-contracts.md)). That is the handoff §Fallback Behavior's resume failure already uses — a typed driver-side condition surfaced on the canonical run-lifecycle surface where consumers already subscribe — so the trip mints no driver event, no new result **status**, and no persisted column. It mints no state transition either: `starting -> failed` and `running -> failed` are both already declared ([Run State Machine §Complete Transition Table](../domain/run-state-machine.md#complete-transition-table)), and those are exactly the two states a provider-bound frame can be in flight in — a run's opening frame can settle before the `starting -> running` transition is recorded, and a steer targets a `running` run by definition. It is emphatically **not** a JSON-RPC error on either path, and the run-start path is the reason the landing had to be asynchronous at all: `startRun` is a daemon-internal lifecycle operation with no wire method behind it, and a run reaching it from the queue or from orchestration has **no caller waiting on a response** — the participant who authored the text may be offline, and a system-originated run has no participant at all. A synchronous refusal has nobody to refuse to. The run's own `run.failed` event is therefore the surface, delivered to whoever is subscribed whenever they subscribe, which is what an asynchronous run outcome means here. An intervention does have a caller, so it answers on its own result branch as well. That intervention settles `degraded` on the existing zero-effect arm — the frame was dispatched, which puts pre-dispatch `rejected` definitionally out of reach ([Queue And Intervention Model §Driver Result To Lifecycle Mapping](../domain/queue-and-intervention-model.md#driver-result-to-lifecycle-mapping)) — with no `fallbackAction`, because no fallback exists: re-sending the same text meets the same parser. It carries the cause **on that response**, through one additive-optional member the driver result envelope gains here: `refusalCode`, a **closed literal union** whose sole V1 member is `driver.text_neutralization_failed`. A closed union rather than a free-form string is the load-bearing choice — the envelope parses untrusted provider output, and a code the daemon selects from a fixed set can never smuggle provider-composed text into an audit record, so this member needs no length bounding and adds no free-form surface. The `status` vocabulary is untouched at `applied | degraded`, the envelope keeps `.strict()` — a **declared** optional key is not an unknown one, which is the only thing strictness rejects — and the daemon's intervention-result mapper reads it onto the caller's response. The member is **best-effort by construction, and the run terminal is the guarantee**: `refusalCode` is present only when the trip is classified before `applyIntervention` resolves. A driver must not hold that call open waiting for its correlated frame to settle — a healthy steer joins a model turn and settles when that turn ends, which can be minutes, so awaiting settlement would convert every ordinary intervention into a long poll and would make the neutralizer's own verification the slowest thing on the intervention path. The asymmetry is what makes the member worth having anyway: the tripped case is the fast one — a zero-turn synthetic reply settles immediately, well inside the call — so the classification is in hand exactly when there is something to report. A consumer that must not miss a trip subscribes to the run: `run.failed` is the surface on both paths and the only one a consumer may depend on, and no reader may treat an absent `refusalCode` as evidence that no trip occurred. Recording the cause twice, on the response and on the run terminal, is deliberate: the caller of a one-shot intervention should not have to subscribe to a run's event stream to learn why its text was not delivered, and the terminal is what a later reader replays. **A tripped intervention fails the run too, and that is worth arguing for, because the opposite reads as the kinder choice.** The tempting argument is that a misfired steer touched nothing, so failing the run destroys work the misfire never harmed — but the hazard is precisely that the provider _dispatched_ the frame, so it may have executed an unrecognized client-side action against the session state the run depends on, and a context clear or a model switch would leave every later turn running against a session this daemon still believes it understands. `recovery-needed` names that exactly: a human reconciles before the run continues. **The provider binding is quarantined with it.** Failing the run alone would not be enough — the run ends, but the provider process holding the possibly-mutated client-side state stays alive, and the next run admitted against that session would bind straight back to it. So the trip also **disposes the run's live provider binding**: the driver closes the provider session, which removes the process from the live provider-process registry, and returning to work means a fresh spawn — which is exactly what _operator restarts the session_ means mechanically. **Disposal is a process fact, not a row state, and deliberately so.** `runtime_bindings` carries no liveness column and must not gain one: [Plan-005](../plans/005-provider-driver-contract-and-capabilities.md)'s binding store returns every row for a run — superseded pre-relaunch history included — and rules that the **caller** owns the liveness intersection against the process supervisor's live provider-process registry before any fan-out or delivery, which is already what keeps a dead or superseded row from reaching a live leg. A disposed binding is therefore excluded from every such path by machinery the store already ships, with no flag for a caller to forget and no column for this rule to mint — and that is also why the refusal takes no attach-time arm: telling _disposed by this trip_ apart from _dead for any other reason_ at attach time would need exactly the persisted marker being refused, which is why [Error Contracts](../architecture/contracts/error-contracts.md) records this code as riding no error envelope on any path. The row itself survives as the history it is, holding the provider resume handle and the realized spawn configuration the audit record needs. **Disposal strands no ratified path, because it creates no new state.** The one exit from a terminal state is a rollback intervention (`failed -> paused`), whose driver leg addresses a live binding for the run — and a terminal run with no live provider process is the **ordinary** case that path already faces: startup failure, resume failure, and an exited provider all reach `failed` with the process gone. This trip leaves the run in exactly that state, so it hands the rollback path no case it did not already have, and the fork-composed `DriverRollbackResult.applied.bindingId` arm exists precisely because such a rollback may return on a binding other than the one the run died on ([Run State Machine §Rollback Transitions](../domain/run-state-machine.md#rollback-transitions-campaign-b2)). What must not be re-entered is the disposed **process**, and no caller can re-enter a process that no longer exists. The driver-level **status** vocabulary stays exactly `applied | degraded`; the one widening this rule makes anywhere on the driver contract is the additive-optional `refusalCode` member above, which no existing caller is required to read and which changes no shipped field's type. Frames of origin `driver_command` are exempt, a command dispatch being their intended outcome. A provider spawn flag claiming to disable command parsing may be set as defense in depth but never in place of the transform or the tripwire — it is the provider's promise rather than the daemon's guarantee, and in `2.1.245` the nearest such flag is described by the binary's own help as disabling skills, a scope that is not the input parse.

- **A provider session is a replay target, not an authority (2026-08-26).** The daemon's canonical transcript is the authority for what a conversation contains ([ADR-029](../decisions/029-canonical-transcript-is-authoritative.md)); a provider's own stored session is a cache of it. Every provider-native continuity verb a driver uses — resume, rewind, fork — is an **optimization behind its capability flag**, and every one has a named daemon-side fallback that reconstitutes the conversation by replay. No driver operation, and no capability built on one, may be specified such that its only path is a provider verb. A driver whose provider cannot be replayed into declares `transcript_replay: false` and runs on the memo projection floor; it does not fail to be a driver.
- **A replay is verified by what the session answers, never by what the call returned (2026-08-26).** Replay frames enter provider surfaces that perform no shape validation on them, so a success return proves only that bytes were accepted. `replayTranscript` therefore completes only after a **post-replay assertion**: the reconstituted session is probed and must answer consistently with the transcript's tail. A session that answers with zero turns, refuses, or contradicts the tail fails the operation — the driver reports the failure and the caller does not receive an `applied` result. This is the §Required Behavior text-neutrality tripwire's discipline applied to a second fail-open surface, and for the same reason: the layer above must not read a completed operation where none occurred.
- **What cannot be carried is declared at the operation that drops it (2026-08-26).** Any operation that reconstitutes a session under a different provider, model, or account emits an explicit list of the facts it could not carry. Provider-private reasoning heads that list by construction ([ADR-029](../decisions/029-canonical-transcript-is-authoritative.md) Assumption 3: it is declared non-portable, not best-effort) — **visible reasoning summaries do carry, as plain text**, because they are participant-visible and therefore already in the canonical record. An empty list is a positive assertion that nothing was dropped, so a driver that does not know what it lost may not emit one. Silence is never an admissible encoding of a loss.
- **A structurally invalid history is a permanent refusal, never a retry (2026-08-26).** Where a provider rejects a request because the conversation it was given is structurally invalid — an unpaired tool call, a reasoning item the vendor's own rules forbid at that position — the rejection is **not transient**, because every subsequent request in that session re-sends the same history and is rejected identically. Retrying it is specified as a defect: it spends money, consumes the run's error budget, and cannot succeed. The driver classifies such a refusal permanent, disposes the run's provider binding, and reconstitutes from the canonical transcript, which is the one record guaranteed not to carry the defect. This is the class the recovery ladder must **not** absorb into `recovery-needed`: a retryable transport fault and a poisoned history look alike from one attempt and differ absolutely from two.
- **Capability delivery follows one preference order, and refusal is a first-class arm (2026-08-26).** Where a capability can be delivered by more than one mechanism, the driver takes the first that is available: **native** provider verb → **daemon-emulated** at the driver boundary → **MCP tool** exposed into the run → **prompt-injected** instruction → **typed refusal**. The order is by fidelity, and the last arm is deliberately a refusal rather than a silent no-op: a capability that cannot be delivered by any of the first four is reported unavailable, never claimed and dropped. §Parity Capability Mechanism Grades records which arm each shipped capability actually lands on, so the grade table is the evidence for this rule rather than a restatement of it.

- **Provider usage counters are cumulative, and what a driver emits is the per-turn delta (2026-08-28).** Both pinned provider surfaces report usage as a **running total for the provider session** rather than a figure for the turn that just completed: the total resets at no turn boundary, at no context compaction, and on no resume (schema shape Verified first-party, runtime behavior Derived — see §References). A normalizer that forwards each reading as though it described one turn therefore re-counts every earlier turn on every later one, and the error compounds with conversation length — measured **22×** overstatement of session spend on a long thread. Because [Spec-016 §Session Cost Receipt](016-multi-agent-channels-and-orchestration.md#session-cost-receipt) folds exactly these rows into the committed-spend figure this product bills and budgets against, the defect lands on money rather than on a display. So: a driver's usage emissions carry **per-axis deltas**, never the provider's cumulative counter. The base is **one register per provider thread and axis, advanced in stream order** as each declared-cumulative reading is consumed — a base is never a snapshot copied at turn dispatch, because two interleaved turns differencing against copies of the same starting value each re-count the interval the other already metered. Attribution is by **the turn the metered frame itself names** (the Codex usage notification carries the naming `turnId` on the frame — see §References), never by whichever turn is open at arrival: a usage frame for one turn routinely lands after the next turn has opened, and the stream-ordered base with named-turn attribution meters that late interval to its own turn instead of flooring it or crediting the newer turn — the same bind-at-dispatch discipline the turn-scoped effective principal already imposes on these same rows ([Spec-006 §Usage Telemetry](006-session-event-taxonomy-and-audit-log.md#usage-telemetry-usage_telemetry)). Base establishment has exactly **two arms**. For a **fresh provider session — a replay-seeded one included — the base is zero and the first reading meters in full**: the provider's counter starts at zero, transcript injection spends nothing, and the first turn's large input is real billed spend that a zero-metered first reading would silently drop. For a **provider-native resume**, the base is **the daemon's own prior-emitted cumulative sum for that thread**, rebuilt from the canonical record ([ADR-029](../decisions/029-canonical-transcript-is-authoritative.md)) — never the first post-resume reading, which would forgive the entire first post-resume turn — so pre-resume spend is never re-metered and the first post-resume interval meters exactly the new spend. Where the wire declares a per-turn figure beside the cumulative one (the Codex breakdown's `last` beside its `total`), the driver consumes it as a **corroborating cross-check** — the derived interval is asserted equal to the declared figure for the naming turn, and a mismatch is recorded as a driver diagnostic — never as a substitute accountant, because the other pinned surface declares no per-turn figure and the declared figure's behavior across resume and compaction is unprobed. A context compaction does **not** re-base: the provider's cumulative counter is unaffected by it, and re-basing there would silently forgive every token spent before the boundary (a compaction's effect on the **window** reading is a separate matter, already specified). A declared-cumulative axis is monotonic non-decreasing within a session, so an observed decrease is a **falsified declaration, not a negative charge**: the emission is floored at zero and the floor-hit is recorded as a driver diagnostic — never emitted as negative spend, and never silently dropped.
- **Normalized token axes partition the turn; no token is counted twice (2026-08-28).** Providers do not agree on whether cache-read tokens sit **inside** the input counter or **beside** it, and both pinned surfaces report a cached-input figure that is a component of their input figure (schema shape Verified first-party, runtime behavior Derived — see §References). A driver that forwards the two as siblings double-counts every cached token; one that forwards the input figure untouched prices cache reads at the uncached rate. The normalize boundary therefore emits a **partition**: the normalized input axis is **uncached input**, with any cached-read component subtracted there, and the normalized output axis is output. Containment is an **arithmetic-checked runtime property, never a schema assumption**: the vendor schema places the cached-input member beside the input member and proves nothing about nesting, so the driver subtracts only where the breakdown's own sum identity confirms the cached component sits inside the input figure, and where no identity holds it emits the input figure unsubtracted and records the failed identity as a driver diagnostic — a conservative overstatement surfaced for repair, never a silent understatement. Cache-read and cache-write remain **separate accounting axes inside the driver**, retained on its diagnostic band so the split is observable rather than discarded; the [Spec-006 §Usage Telemetry](006-session-event-taxonomy-and-audit-log.md#usage-telemetry-usage_telemetry) payload registers no per-cache-axis member and this spec mints none — a declaration minted ahead of the consumer that reads it decays in silence, which is the reasoning §Capability discovery states for capability flags and which applies unchanged here. That is a **stated limit, not a silence**: a consumer needing the components separately is an ordinary `usage_telemetry` growth for Spec-006 to register, and the driver holds the values meanwhile. One consequence binds immediately — a driver that derives cost prices from the **provider's full breakdown**, the same reading it normalized from and at each component's own rate, never by re-multiplying the normalized axes, which are a telemetry partition and not a billing basis.
- **A provider connection carries more than one thread, and only the session's own thread projects (2026-08-28).** Both pinned providers multiplex **child-thread** traffic — subagent, review, and compaction threads the provider spawns for itself — over the same connection that carries the session's own thread (schema shape Verified first-party, runtime behavior Derived — see §References; corroborated in-tree by a shipped third-party integration that surfaces Codex review, compaction, and thread-spawn children under a parent and hides memory-consolidation children outright). A normalizer that projects every frame it receives renders a child's assistant output and tool activity into the parent's timeline as though the parent produced them — a fabricated transcript — and it either double-counts or loses the child's spend. So: every inbound frame is first classified by its **family** against the pinned stream-surface census, and the routing obligation is **family-scoped**. A **thread-scoped** family routes by explicit thread identity read from the frame, and only frames carrying the session's own thread identity project into that session's timeline; a **connection- or account-scoped** family the census registers — the retry, rate-limit, and capability/initialization surfaces that structurally carry no thread identity — routes to its registered normalization without one, because demanding a member the frame's own shape excludes would quarantine every account-plane signal the corpus already consumes. A frame of an **unrecognized family** carrying no thread identity is still quarantined: the census is the discriminator, and an unlisted shape is never presumed connection-scoped. Thread identities enter the recognized set on a **registration path, ahead of the refusal rule**: the session's own thread registers at establishment, and a child identity registers from the provider's own **parent-linked announcement** — the Codex thread-start notification carrying the parent thread identity and a subagent source kind, the Claude subagent-start signal arriving in the parent's own stream with the parent linkage on it (see §References) — so recognition derives from the provider's declared lineage, never from arrival order. Routing is then **fail-closed** with two distinct held states, both bounded. A frame naming a thread that is **present but not yet registered** — child traffic racing its own announcement — enters a **pending-registration hold**: released into ordinary routing when the registration lands, shed with a recorded diagnostic when a declared timeout passes without one. A frame whose thread identity is **absent where its family requires one, or names a thread no registration path admits**, is **quarantined and reported as a driver diagnostic** — never projected, never silently dropped, and never attributed to the parent by arrival order or position. The quarantine is a diagnostic buffer rather than a queue awaiting delivery, and both it and the pending hold shed oldest-first under declared bounds rather than growing without limit. Two carve-outs bind **before suppression**. Usage counters are carved out first, so child spend is metered even though child content is not — spend the provider attributes to a subagent is attributed to that subagent on the already-registered (`runId`, `provider`, `subagentId`) triple, and spend on a provider-internal child that carries no subagent identity (a compaction or memory-consolidation thread) attributes to the **parent run at run scope**, which is what it is: real spend incurred on the session's behalf by machinery the participant did not name. Neither arm invents a subagent and neither drops a token. Second, a child thread's **interactive requests** — tool-approval, permission, and input requests the provider raises from inside a child — are carved out and routed through the **same dispatch and approval pipeline as the parent run's**, answered on the child's own correlation identity: [Spec-016 §Provider-Native Subagents](016-multi-agent-channels-and-orchestration.md#provider-native-subagents) requires exactly this ("subagent tool calls flow through the same approval pipeline as the parent run's; a subagent introduces no separate trust surface"), and suppressing the request with the transcript would not hide the child but hang it — the provider blocks awaiting an answer no layer will send. What suppression governs is the child's **transcript projection alone**. Child **lifecycle** reaches the timeline only through the already-registered `subagent.started` / `subagent.completed` kinds; a child thread's own frames are never a second lifecycle channel.

- **Context compaction is participant-triggerable and never automatic (2026-08-29).** A driver declaring `context_compaction` implements `compactContext`, which asks the provider to compact the bound session's own context on a participant's explicit request. The runtime **never** triggers it on a threshold, a timer, or a heuristic: the [Spec-013 §Context Window and Usage Meters](013-live-timeline-visibility-and-reasoning-surfaces.md#context-window-and-usage-meters) hint stays informational, and this operation is a second thing beside it rather than the automation that bullet forbids. The operation **settles on the provider's own typed compaction evidence** — the frame that already produces the registered `usage.context_compacted` boundary — and never on the request being accepted, because both delivery mechanisms below can be accepted and then do nothing. It is capability-gated with no emulation arm: a driver that cannot compact declares `false` and refuses as `driver.capability_unsupported`. Prompt-injected self-summarization is **prohibited** as a substitute — it does not move the provider's own context window, so it would report success for work that did not happen, which §Capability delivery's typed-refusal arm exists precisely to prevent. Compaction moves no delta base (§Required Behavior's per-turn delta rule already states that a compaction is not a re-base point), and its boundary is what [Spec-004](004-queue-steer-pause-resume.md)'s compaction-boundary-aware rewind classifies against, unchanged.
- **An enumerated provider command belongs to the binding it was read from (2026-08-29).** A driver declaring `provider_commands` implements `listProviderCommands`, and every entry it returns is bound to the `(driverName, providerAccountId)` pair the enumeration was read under. The binding is a **routing invariant**, not a label: an entry is offerable only to agents of that binding and dispatchable only through them. A command read from a Claude session must never be offered to, or sent through, a Codex agent — the two providers share a `/name` syntax and share nothing else, so a cross-provider dispatch is not a degraded call but a different command or none, and it refuses as `driver.capability_unsupported` rather than being attempted. The enumeration is a **live read held as driver-session state**: it is not persisted, not cached across sessions, and not folded into any projection, so a definition a participant adds or removes between sessions is picked up by the next read rather than by an invalidation protocol this corpus would then have to own. Where a provider announces that its own set changed, the driver re-reads rather than patching its held copy.
- **The output-speed axis is single-provider and declared, never inferred (2026-08-29).** A driver declaring `output_speed` reports the provider's own declared speed state and the provider's own reason when that state is not the requested one. The flag's detection source is **`static`**, not `probed`: reading the declared state on the pinned Claude surface costs a user-message request, and §Capability discovery admits a probe only where it is zero-turn, non-mutating, and decisive, so the honest declaration is a static one from the driver's per-driver table. **The provider's declared state is the truth, and a request is not.** Where a provider accepts the driver's opt-in and its own account or settings gate then denies the mode, the declared state stays `off` and the driver reports `off` with the provider's reason — never the requested value, and never a synthesized success. The two values therefore have **two separate carriers and neither substitutes for the other**: the requested mode travels on the spawn (a fresh create and a resume alike, reconstructed on recovery from the durable spawn record), and the declared state travels back on the handshake that spawn already performs, read live and stored in no column. A requested mode with no spawn carrier would be committed to the agent row and never told to the process; a declared state written to a column would outlive the session that observed it. **A `false` cell is a declaration about this contract's axis, not a census of the provider's wire (corrected 2026-08-31).** A driver declares `output_speed: false` when its provider supplies no _declarable_ axis — no statically declarable value vocabulary and no declared-state read — and that is a complete and honest declaration rather than a gap, whether or not some settable parameter with adjacent semantics exists on the wire. The pinned Codex surface is exactly that case and is why the rule is stated this way rather than as a negative: it carries a participant-settable per-turn service tier and a per-model tier catalog and still supplies neither conjunct (§The output-speed axis). The analogous control on such a provider is the `model` / `effort` axis [Spec-016 §Same-Agent Provider Switch](016-multi-agent-channels-and-orchestration.md#same-agent-provider-switch) already carries, and no speed axis is emulated onto it.

- **An answer that will not fit the wire is refused, never truncated (2026-08-30).** A provider-raised ask — a callback tool call, a permission or input request, an MCP elicitation — is answered back over the transport that carried it, and that transport bounds what one outbound frame may carry. Every driver that answers such asks MUST apply an explicit **outbound** frame bound; where the provider publishes no figure, the honest default is the bound that same transport already applies to that wire in the inbound direction, and the bound is measured on the **encoded** frame the write step will actually send rather than estimated from the composed object — an estimate being a second, disagreeing implementation of the serializer. Where a composed answer exceeds the bound, the driver MUST answer with that ask's **own refusal shape** carrying a bounded typed reason, and MUST NOT send a truncated answer: a truncated tool result is a wrong answer the model cannot distinguish from a complete one, where a refusal is an answer the model can act on. Two properties close the rule. The refusal reason is a **constant**, never a slice of what overflowed — a reason built from the oversized payload can overflow again, reintroducing by its own remedy the failure the bound exists to prevent — so the substitute fits by construction. And every attempt that does not reach the provider is **recorded**: an oversized answer, an unencodable one, and a rejected write each emit a driver diagnostic, so an ask this driver cannot answer is never _silently_ unanswered. A write rejection in particular may not be swallowed on the theory that the exit path reports it — the exit path records that the process died, not that a specific ask went unanswered, and a write can reject on a closed connection while the ask's caller is still waiting on an answer that will now never come.

## Default Behavior

- Driver capability declarations are required at attach time and may be refreshed when provider state changes.
- Initial provider drivers are local-runtime-node integrations. They may call remote provider APIs or services, but driver control and execution authority remain attached to the local runtime node.
- Driver transport defaults to `stdio`. The Codex driver may additionally be configured — config-gated, off by default — to attach over `unix://` or `ws://` app-server listeners with bearer auth (`DriverTransportConfig`; the credential is held as a daemon-config reference, never inline). The Claude CLI exposes no local listener; remote Claude participation is cross-node dispatch per [Spec-024](024-cross-node-dispatch-and-approval.md) — recorded as the parity mechanism, not a driver transport.
- Unknown capability fields are ignored (tolerant reader). The declared `contractVersion` is a change-detection signal — the daemon records it at attach and compares it on refresh to detect contract drift and invalidate capability snapshots — not a negotiation surface: the daemon never version-gates its behavior on it, and additive contract evolution with tolerant reading replaces negotiated feature-unlocking.
- The runtime must only surface controls that correspond to supported capabilities for the active run.

## Fallback Behavior

- If a driver cannot resume a previously persisted handle, it must surface `provider failure` detail and a visible `recovery-needed` condition; it must not silently create a replacement provider session under the same canonical run. Recovery conditions form the closed `RecoveryCondition` set — `recovery-needed` (generic: operator reconciliation required) and `reauth-required` (the provider session or credential expired: detected mid-run via the provider's typed auth-failure signals or at resume/probe time; remediation is re-authenticating the provider CLI on the runtime node, after which recovery may retry). The named type is declared once in [API Payload Contracts](../architecture/contracts/api-payload-contracts.md) and referenced at every carrying surface.
- **Provider usage limits are a sibling axis, not a recovery condition (2026-08-18).** When a provider refuses or defers a turn because the bound account's plan allowance is exhausted, the driver surfaces a typed **provider usage-limit signal**, distinct from `RecoveryCondition` and never folded into it: a `recovery-needed` condition asks a human to reconcile, whereas a usage limit resolves on its own at a known boundary and asks the runtime to wait. The signal carries a **closed-set cause** and an **optional reset boundary**; the boundary carries its own provenance, distinguishing a value the provider stated from one the runtime derived, because a derived boundary may be wrong and a consumer that cannot tell will present a guess as a fact. Recognition is **typed-only**: a driver emits the signal solely from a structured provider event whose shape it can name, and MUST NOT infer a usage limit by matching message text, exit codes, or HTTP status alone. An unrecognized or unparseable shape yields **no signal** — the honest absence — rather than a synthesized one; a consumer reads that as "not known to be limited", never as "known not to be limited". A driver that cannot observe usage limits at all declares nothing here and is not emulated: this is the parity triad's degrade-honestly arm, and the pacing consumer's behavior without a signal is unchanged from today's.
- On successful resume the driver reports `sessionPosition` — its normalized monotonic position (turn/event ordinal) for the resumed provider session. The daemon compares it against the recorded position; reconciliation semantics (divergence halts for human decision) are Spec-015's, landing via campaign B5 per [ADR-017](../decisions/017-shared-event-sourcing-scope.md)'s local-log-authoritative ruling. A provider that silently returns a fresh session on resume (for example on a working-directory mismatch) is caught by this comparison rather than silently adopted.
- If a provider offers provider-native data that cannot be normalized safely, the runtime must store it as diagnostic metadata only, not as canonical domain state.
- If a driver does not support dynamic model or mode mutation, the runtime must require a new run or agent configuration rather than simulating mutation.
- If a requested integration path would require shared hosted execution outside the local runtime boundary, v1 must reject or mark that path unsupported rather than routing execution through the collaboration control plane.

## Interfaces And Contracts

- Required driver operations:
  - `createSession`
  - `resumeSession`
  - `startRun`
  - `interruptRun`
  - `applyIntervention` — generic dispatcher for steer, interrupt, cancel; checks capability flags and returns `degraded` for unsupported intervention types. Its result envelope carries one **additive-optional** `refusalCode` member (2026-08-25, §Required Behavior's text-neutrality tripwire) — a closed literal union, `driver.text_neutralization_failed` its sole V1 member — set when a dispatched frame's neutralization did not hold. The `status` union is unchanged, the envelope stays `.strict()`, and every existing caller keeps working without reading it.
  - `rollbackTo` — conversation rollback to a normalized session position on the run's **live provider binding** (`RollbackToParams {sessionId, position, bindingId}` — the `bindingId` leg key is daemon-resolved at dispatch, the same per-binding addressing as goal delivery, because run→bindings is 1:many; typed shape + resolution rule in [API Payload Contracts](../architecture/contracts/api-payload-contracts.md), campaign B2; gated on the `rollback` flag; file-state restore is the daemon's turn-snapshot leg, [Plan-010](../plans/010-worktree-lifecycle-and-execution-modes.md), never the driver's)
  - `respondToRequest`
  - `setSessionGoal` / `clearSessionGoal` — session-goal injection (gated on `session_goals`; the daemon supplies the rendered goal text and the target `bindingId` (+ `runId`) — delivery is per-binding (`run` → bindings is 1:many; one call per live binding, the fan-out ack unit) — the structured goal shape is owned by the Spec-016 goal contract, campaign B6)
  - `closeSession`
  - `listModels`
  - `listModes`
  - `getCapabilities`
  - `probeAuth` — zero-turn authentication probe (required of every driver)
  - `exportTranscript` — render the run's canonical transcript into this provider's replay frames (2026-08-26). Input is the daemon-supplied canonical transcript projection and a target boundary; output is provider-shaped frames plus the **declared-loss list** for anything the target could not represent. Pure with respect to session state: exporting mutates nothing and starts no turn.
  - `replayTranscript` — reconstitute a conversation into a **fresh** provider session from exported frames (2026-08-26, gated on `transcript_replay`). Returns only after the post-replay assertion passes; a driver whose provider refuses prior-turn content declares the flag `false` and the daemon falls back to the memo projection with the operation reported `degraded`. Replay never re-mints tool-call identifiers (§Canonical Transcript Export And Replay) and never writes to the source session.
  - `compactContext` — ask the provider to compact the bound session's own context (2026-08-29, gated on `context_compaction`). Operates on an already-established session and starts no participant turn. Settles only on the provider's typed compaction evidence, never on the request being accepted (§Participant-triggered context compaction). A driver declaring the flag `false` refuses as `driver.capability_unsupported`; nothing is emulated by prompt injection, because a prompt asking a model to summarize itself does not move the provider's own context window and would report success for work that did not happen. Its wait for that evidence is **bounded** and ends at whichever comes first of the driver's declared per-binding compaction bound or the run's provider binding ceasing to be live; expiry settles `failed`, never `applied`, and a compaction frame arriving afterward is still normalized into `usage.context_compacted`. The result is a **discriminated union** on its settlement — `applied` carrying the boundary position (`null` where the provider's frame states none), `refused` where nothing was sent, `failed` where something was sent and no boundary was witnessed. Authorized by the identical `Action::"intervene"` adjudication the intervention path runs, on the run owning the named binding; no Cedar action and no error code is minted for it.
  - `listProviderCommands` — read the provider's own enumeration of native slash-commands and skills for the bound session (2026-08-29, gated on `provider_commands`). A **live read** returning the enumeration as the driver observed it, held as driver-session state and discarded with the session; it is deliberately not a registry, not persisted, and not a projection the daemon folds. Every returned entry carries the `(driverName, providerAccountId)` binding it was read from (§The provider command and skill surface). The enumeration is a **discovery** surface in V1 and not a dispatch channel — exactly one enumerated entry is sent, the compaction command through `compactContext` — and reading it requires active session membership and nothing more.
- Two of the eighteen operations above were minted 2026-08-29, and the driver-operation surface is **eighteen** as of that date.
- Required normalized event families:
  - run lifecycle
  - assistant output
  - tool activity
  - interactive request
  - artifact publication
  - usage or quota telemetry where available
- Normalization obligations bind at the driver's normalize boundary: usage/quota telemetry is provenance-stamped by the driver — it sets `windowSource` (`provider_reported | model_default | estimated`) and computes the `exceeded` headroom predicate at normalization ([Spec-006 §Usage Telemetry](006-session-event-taxonomy-and-audit-log.md#usage-telemetry-usage_telemetry)) — never deferring provenance or the derived predicate to consumers.
- **Provider-account identity is an additive-optional spawn input (2026-08-18).** Session-creation and resume parameters carry an **optional** provider-account identifier. Omitting it is a supported, unchanged path: the daemon resolves the provider's default account, and a caller written before this amendment behaves exactly as it did. The identifier is **opaque to the driver** — it selects a credential environment and is never parsed, decomposed, or used to locate credential material by the driver itself. The daemon constructs that environment and hands it to the spawn; a driver MUST NOT read ambient provider credentials from the operator's own environment or home directory when an account is bound, because doing so would silently execute against a different account than the one the run was admitted for, and the resulting spend would be attributed to an account that never paid it. The identifier is **server-resolved and server-stamped**: a client-supplied value is an input to resolution, never the recorded outcome. Resume rebinds to the account the run was originally admitted against rather than re-resolving the current default, so a default changed mid-session cannot silently move an in-flight run's billing. Registration, defaults, credential-home layout, validation, and concurrency posture are Spec-029's; this bullet is the whole of the driver-facing seam.
- **Capability detection source rides the capability report (2026-08-26).** Every flag `getCapabilities` reports carries a closed-literal `CapabilityDetectionSource` — `static` (declared from the driver's own per-driver table) or `probed` (decided against the installed build by a zero-turn probe). It is an **additive-optional**, **live-scoped**, report-only member of the **driver-side** read — present and total over the flag set on a live driver read, absent exactly when the wrapper was reconstructed from the durable capability cache, so its absence reads as _cache reconstruction_ rather than as _unknown provenance_ and a consumer needing provenance re-reads the driver. It rides `GetCapabilitiesResult` — registered on that wrapper beside `cliVersion` in [api-payload-contracts.md §Plan-005 — Provider Driver Contract (Internal Interface)](../architecture/contracts/api-payload-contracts.md#plan-005--provider-driver-contract-internal-interface) in this same swap — and, exactly as the mechanism grades and `cliVersion` itself do, it stops there: this member does not reach the registered client-facing `driver.listCapabilities` payload, and a consumer that needs a flag's provenance reads it through the daemon rather than off that reply, per §Capability discovery. It is deliberately **not** persisted and **not** mirrored into the `CapabilityDetails` event payload — on `cliVersion`'s own precedent there, provenance is not a per-snapshot capability property — `driver_capabilities` caches the flag values the change-detection compare reads, and provenance is a property of the reading rather than of the cached row, so no column, no CHECK widening, and no migration ordinal is consumed. No operation is minted and no client-facing payload is widened **by this member**: what grows is the driver-side wrapper the daemon already reads, per §Capability discovery. (The sibling `outputSpeedLevels` that 2026-08-29 adds to the same wrapper is deliberately the other way round — it is served to clients, because its reader is a participant-facing control rather than a daemon consumer; §Provider Parameter Vocabularies states why the two differ.)
- **Usage-delta and thread-routing obligations bind at the same normalize boundary (2026-08-28).** The per-turn delta derivation, the non-overlapping token partition, and the thread-identity routing of every inbound frame are normalization duties, discharged exactly where `windowSource` and `exceeded` already are — before an event reaches the session engine. A consumer never receives a cumulative counter to difference, an overlapping axis to reconcile, or a foreign-thread frame to filter, and none of the three may be deferred to one. The per-thread delta bases, the thread-identity registry, and the pending-registration hold are **driver-session state**: held for the life of the provider session and discarded with it, with each base re-established on resume from the daemon's own prior-emitted cumulative sum per §Required Behavior — never from the first post-resume reading.

### Parity Capability Mechanism Grades

Boolean flags answer **whether** the driver delivers a capability; mechanism grades record **how** the system delivers it end-to-end (`native | emulated | absent`), generalizing the `mcp: true` honesty rule above. ### Provider Parameter Vocabularies

**Effort vocabularies are per-model and provider-published, never per-provider (corrected 2026-08-30).** The vocabulary a driver may offer for a model is the list that model publishes on the provider's own catalog read, carried verbatim on `ProviderModel.effortLevels`; **absent means the model exposes no effort selection at all**, which is a reading rather than an omission. Codex accepts per-turn model/effort/sandbox/approval overrides (`turn/start` params — the daemon surfaces per-turn posture via `StartRunParams.executionPosture`; other per-turn knobs ride `agentConfig`), where Claude binds these per session at spawn.

## Canonical Transcript Export And Replay

Added 2026-08-26 under [ADR-029](../decisions/029-canonical-transcript-is-authoritative.md). This section owns the driver-boundary contract for reconstituting a conversation; the capabilities that _invoke_ it — rollback ([Spec-004](004-queue-steer-pause-resume.md)), recovery ([Spec-015](015-persistence-recovery-and-replay.md)), cross-node dispatch ([Spec-024](024-cross-node-dispatch-and-approval.md)), and the same-agent provider switch ([Spec-016 §Same-Agent Provider Switch](016-multi-agent-channels-and-orchestration.md#same-agent-provider-switch)) — each own their own trigger and their own settlement.

### The canonical transcript is a projection, never a store

The canonical transcript is **derived from the session event log on demand and rebuilt every turn**. It is not a table, not a file, and not a one-shot migration performed once at switch time. This is the whole reason ADR-029 rejected a separate durable transcript store: a second record of facts the log already holds must then be kept in step with it, which is the divergence problem the decision exists to eliminate, relocated inside the daemon. A consumer that caches a rendered transcript across turns is caching a projection whose source has moved.

Its content is bounded by [Spec-006](006-session-event-taxonomy-and-audit-log.md): the normalized event taxonomy is the complete set of facts a driver may report, and therefore the complete set the transcript can carry. Anything a provider held that never became an event is, by construction, not in the canonical record — which is what the declared-loss rule exists to surface.

### The transform pipeline is ordered, and the order is load-bearing

`exportTranscript` applies these steps in this sequence. Reordering them is a defect, not a style choice:

1. **Fold** the run's normalized events into ordered turns.
2. **Map identity.** Every tool call keeps a stable, bidirectional mapping between the canonical id and the id the target provider sees. Replay never re-mints a tool-call identifier and never reuses one across two distinct calls — the general form of this spec's standing prohibition on letting provider-native ids replace canonical ids (§Pitfalls To Avoid).
3. **Strip what is not portable**, and record each stripped class on the declared-loss list. Provider-private reasoning is stripped here, on **both** vendors' own stated rules: reasoning is tied to the model that produced it, and is reusable at most within one model family. Stripping is by the reasoning block's kind, not by a single type name — a filter that catches only the plain reasoning kind silently leaves its redacted sibling behind and breaks the multi-turn protocol. Visible reasoning summaries are **not** stripped; they carry as plain text, because they are participant-visible and therefore already canonical.

   The strip is **unconditional**, including on a same-provider, same-model replay where a vendor's own reasoning signatures would in principle still validate. Carrying them would look like a free win and is not one: a signature is validated against the block's **position** as well as its bytes, so a replay that preserved them would owe an exact reproduction of block order and count — a second, provider-specific correctness contract layered underneath the canonical one, whose failures surface as opaque signature rejections rather than as declared losses. That is precisely the divergence [ADR-029](../decisions/029-canonical-transcript-is-authoritative.md) exists to eliminate, so the pipeline declines the win and declares the loss.

   The strip is also why a switch is applied at a turn boundary **after the target model's last assistant turn has completed**: stale reasoning on _prior_ assistant turns is tolerated (a token cost), while modifying the _latest_ assistant turn's reasoning sequence is a hard refusal. The boundary default in [Spec-016 §Same-Agent Provider Switch](016-multi-agent-channels-and-orchestration.md#same-agent-provider-switch) is therefore a correctness rule, not only a courtesy to work in flight.

4. **Repair pairing integrity**, strictly after the strip. A tool call whose result was removed, or whose result never existed because the turn was interrupted, receives a **synthetic error result** naming the interruption. It is never dropped, and it is never left unpaired: the target's injection surface performs no pairing validation, so an unpaired call is accepted silently and the reconstituted session then carries tool history that does not match what happened.
5. **Render** into the target's frame shapes, with every outbound frame declaring its origin per §Required Behavior's frame-origin discriminator — a replayed participant turn is `participant_text` and is neutralized exactly as a live one is.

Step 4 running before step 3 would repair pairs the strip then breaks. Step 2 running after step 5 would render ids the map has not yet fixed. The sequence is the contract.

### The memo projection is the floor, and it is visibly a floor

Where `transcript_replay` is `false`, where the target refuses the frames, or where the transcript exceeds the target's context window, the daemon falls back to the **memo projection**: a bounded prose rendering of the same canonical transcript, sent once, framed as continuing an existing conversation rather than as a new instruction.

Four properties are normative, and the last is the one implementations get wrong:

- **A budget expressed as a fraction of the target's context window**, not an absolute token count, so the same rule holds across models with different windows.
- **A protected tail of the most recent tool exchanges that the budget may not evict**, and eviction operates on **whole exchanges**: a call and its result travel together, so a tight budget can never leave one half of an exchange in the memo and the other half out.
- **Once-only delivery, enforced by a _derived_ memo-identity key rather than by a durable claim.** The obvious design — a claim written before the send and released on failure — needs a durable owner, and there is none to give it: `runtime_bindings` is ruled out for recovery state, and no generic claim store exists in the local schema, so the claim would have to mint a table to hold one in-flight marker for something the event log already determines. The key is therefore **computed, not stored**: a deterministic function of the canonical transcript projection at the export log position and the target session's identity. Because that transcript is a projection rebuilt from the log rather than a store, the same switch yields the same key on any daemon and across any restart — the key is recoverable exactly when it is needed, and **no daemon-side claim row is persisted**. Derivation is only half of the mechanism, though, and the other half is what makes reconciliation possible at all: the key is also **carried on the memo turn itself**, as a visible marker in the sent prose — kept legible to the human reading the target conversation rather than hidden in an invisible or zero-width sentinel, which this spec prohibits outright — because a key that were derived and never sent would leave nothing in the target to match against. Derivation supplies the key without storing it locally; carriage supplies the value to compare it to; both legs are load-bearing and neither alone yields once-only delivery. **Every send reconciles first**: recompute the key and read the target session's own recent turns for it, treating a match as delivered. That makes the ambiguous send unremarkable rather than special — a timeout, a dropped connection, or a daemon restart between send and acknowledgment leaves the outcome unknown, and the unknown resolves exactly as the known cases do, by reading the target. Rollback-then-retry is not merely discouraged but **unrepresentable**, there being no claim to roll back, which is the point: releasing a claim on an ambiguous send is how the same memo gets injected twice. Where the target cannot be read back, nothing is sent and the operation settles on what did land, because a duplicated memo corrupts the conversation the participant is watching while a missing one degrades it visibly. A driver whose native replay succeeds **suppresses** the memo entirely rather than sending both. This diverges deliberately from the convergent prior art in §References, which pairs an atomic claim with rollback: that design is correct where a durable claim has an owner, and derivation is what stands in for the owner here.
- **Rebuilt from the log every turn**, like the transcript itself, rather than pinned at fallback time.

These are not derived from first principles — they are the shape independent implementations converged on, and each property exists because its absence is a known defect (§References).

Every operation settled on the memo projection reports `degraded`, never `applied`, and carries the declared-loss list. A degraded settlement that renders identically to an applied one is a defect: the participant is entitled to know that the conversation the model can see is a summary.

### Capability discovery

`getCapabilities` is the driver-side read for everything in §Per-Driver Capability Matrix and §Parity Capability Mechanism Grades, and it is the only source a caller may consult: a capability is available when the driver declares it, never when a method exists on the provider's wire. The two are not the same set — the pinned Codex surface carries a method that is present, callable, and upstream-deprecated, and the pinned Claude binary carries a flag string for a feature removed several versions earlier ([Claude wire reference §CLI / wire surface](../reference/provider-wire/claude.md#cli--wire-surface)). Presence is not availability.

The obligation runs in the other direction too: **a capability flag is minted together with the consumer that reads it, never ahead of one.** A declared flag is a promise a caller may plan against, so one gating a capability no surface in this corpus takes is a promise no code keeps — and it decays in silence, because the reader that would have caught it wrong never arrives. A capability the corpus does not yet consume is therefore recorded in prose with its fallback, and its flag is minted in the amendment that mints its first reader.

**Declaration is a reading of the installed build, not of a table (2026-08-26).** §Required Behavior binds every flag carrying an **admissible** probe — zero-turn, non-mutating, and decisive at the granularity the capability is consumed at — to be `probed` and to carry that detection source on the report. The consequence for this section is that the two directions above are not symmetric in strength: presence is not availability, and **absence is not unavailability** either — a censused registry bounds availability from below, not above. So a driver may not resolve a flag from a version comparison or from a census, and it may not resolve from this spec's matrix any flag carrying an admissible probe; it resolves those by probing and classifying the answer. The matrix remains the resolution path for **every** flag the mechanism table **declares** non-probeable, each with its failing admissibility conjunct named — and that set is the large majority rather than a pair, **re-derived by counting on 2026-08-30**. Over this spec's **seventeen** flags across both drivers the table holds **thirty-four** entries, of which **four** are `probed` and **thirty** are `static`. The four probeable entries are all on the Codex leg — `steer`, `session_goals`, `context_compaction`, and `provider_commands`, each decided by the accepted-method enumeration at the granularity its consumer calls it — and the Claude leg declares **zero**, which is a measured finding rather than an omission: its one zero-turn channel refuses the outbound-raised subtypes _by name_, so a refusal there is evidence about the inbound dispatcher and not about the capability. The corollary for §Acceptance Criteria is stated rather than left implicit: the negative control AC14 requires stays **declared and dormant** on a leg that declares no probe, and re-arms the moment an entry there becomes `probed`. Static entries fall into four failing-conjunct shapes and each names its own: `mcp` on **both** drivers fails **zero-turn and non-mutating together** (the only direct probe of it is invoking a tool, which costs a turn and performs the tool's own effect); Claude `model_mutation` fails **non-mutating** alone; Claude `context_compaction`, `provider_commands`, and `output_speed` fail **zero-turn**, their evidence riding a turn-bearing session handshake; and the remaining **twenty-four** fail **decisive-at-consumption-granularity**, the Codex rollback boundary among them, where the enumeration establishes the method and not the parameter. The set-replacing MCP server mutation is **not** among them, because it is not a capability flag at all — it is a control-request subtype no driver-side probe may issue, and its availability is the consuming plan's own reconcile answer. The matrix and the mechanism grades remain what the corpus plans against and what a refresh compares to; they are not what a running driver reads its own answer from.

The client-facing read of the flags is the already-registered `driver.listCapabilities`, and **no method is minted here**: it is one of the client-facing `driver.*` names [Plan-005 §Namespace](../plans/005-provider-driver-contract-and-capabilities.md) registers into the daemon IPC namespace [Spec-007](007-local-ipc-and-daemon-control.md) owns, and widening what `getCapabilities` reports widens what that read serves without changing its name. That set stood at **seven** names from its ratification until 2026-08-29, when `driver.compactContext` and `driver.listProviderCommands` took it to **nine** — the two verbs this spec's desktop-console parity amendment mints, admitted by the same governing principle that fixed the set at seven (both operate on an already-existing session or run; neither establishes, restores, starts, or tears one down) rather than as exceptions to it. The mechanism grades are the driver-side read's: `getCapabilities` reports flags and grades alike, but the registered client-facing payload carries the flags, and a consumer that needs a grade reads it through the daemon rather than off that reply. The driver-side obligation — that `getCapabilities` reports both, refreshed on the §Resolved Questions cadence — is what this spec owns.

## Desktop Console Parity Surfaces

Added 2026-08-29. Three capabilities the pinned provider consoles expose to their own interactive users and this driver contract did not carry, so a participant working through this product had strictly less control than the same participant working through the vendor's terminal. Each is capability-gated, each mints its flag **together with the consumer that reads it** as §Capability discovery requires — the composer affordances and settings surfaces named per section below, all landing in the same swap — and none of the three introduces a table, a column, or an error code.

### Participant-triggered context compaction

`compactContext` compacts the bound session's provider-side context on a participant's explicit request, and only on one. The distinction from the meter hint is the whole point of the operation: [Spec-013 §Context Window and Usage Meters](013-live-timeline-visibility-and-reasoning-surfaces.md#context-window-and-usage-meters) has always said the warning is informational and that compaction is not triggered automatically, and that statement is **unchanged and still normative**. Automation remains prohibited. What changes is that the participant now has a control to reach for, which is a different thing from the system deciding on their behalf, and the two are stated separately so neither can be read as the other.

**Settlement is on evidence, never on acceptance.** Both delivery mechanisms — Codex's `thread/compact/start` and Claude's `/compact` — return before the work is done: the former answers an empty ack, the latter is a command frame whose settling says only that bytes were taken. So the operation completes on the provider's own typed compaction frame, the same frame that already produces the registered `usage.context_compacted` boundary. A driver that dispatches and cannot observe that frame reports the operation failed; it never reports a compaction it did not witness. A compaction moves **no** delta base (§Required Behavior's per-turn delta rule), and the boundary it produces is what [Spec-004](004-queue-steer-pause-resume.md)'s compaction-boundary-aware rewind classifies against — this operation gives that classifier a new way to be reached, not a new thing to classify.

**The wait for that evidence is bounded, and it has two terminals rather than one.** Settling on evidence is only safe if not-settling ends: a provider that acknowledges and then produces nothing would otherwise leave the operation — and the control the participant is watching — open forever. So the wait ends at whichever comes first of the driver's **declared per-binding compaction bound** (the same declared-timeout shape the child-frame pending-registration hold already uses in §Required Behavior, and a per-driver declaration rather than a corpus constant because the two mechanisms do not take comparable time) and the run's **provider binding ceasing to be live** — a process exit or a disposal, which is a positive observation that the evidence is never coming and should not be waited out. Expiry settles **`failed`**, never `applied`, and records a diagnostic naming which terminal ended it; the operation never reports a compaction it did not witness, which is the rule this bound protects rather than relaxes.

Bounding the **operation** does not bound the **boundary's record**, and conflating the two would lose data. A typed compaction frame arriving after the wait ended is normalized into `usage.context_compacted` exactly as an unsolicited provider-initiated compaction is — the driver has no privileged "this one was requested" path, and a late frame is indistinguishable from a spontaneous one at the point of normalization anyway. So no boundary escapes [Spec-004](004-queue-steer-pause-resume.md)'s rewind classifier because a timer expired, and the participant's control is told the truth it had at settlement time. The consumer side follows from the same split: [Spec-013 §Context Window and Usage Meters](013-live-timeline-visibility-and-reasoning-surfaces.md#context-window-and-usage-meters)'s in-progress state clears on the **operation's** settlement, so the control cannot hang waiting for a frame that will arrive on its own schedule or not at all, while the meter itself moves on the boundary row whenever that row lands.

**Authorization is the intervention adjudication, reused rather than reinvented.** Compaction irreversibly mutates the bound run's provider-side context for every participant in the session, so it is not a read and it is not ungated. A caller who is not an active participant of the session is refused indistinguishably from a caller naming a session that does not exist — the registered `session.not_found`, whose description is already "does not exist or is not accessible" — so the operation is no session-existence oracle. A participant is then adjudicated by the **identical `Action::"intervene"` evaluation** the intervention path runs, on the run that owns the named binding, reading the [Security Architecture §Permission Matrix](../architecture/security-architecture.md#permission-matrix-task-54) run-control row — whose subject this amendment extends to name compaction, so the citation lands on a row that names the operation rather than on one a reader has to extend by inference; the matrix gains no row, which is the same claim as reusing the action. That reuse is the `run.pause` / `run.resume` precedent exactly: those are not interventions either, and they share this adjudication rather than minting their own. **No Cedar action is minted and no error code is minted** — compaction shares the adjudication, not the envelope, so [Spec-004](004-queue-steer-pause-resume.md)'s deliberately closed V1 control set is untouched and the driver error namespace does not move. The non-local hosting-node approval leg that adjudication also carries is unreachable here by construction rather than waived: a provider binding this daemon holds is hosted on this node, so the resolved hosting node is always local. A denial settles on the operation's own **refusal arm** — the shape an authorization-rejected intervention already uses, riding its result's reason rather than a transport error — so a viewer who reaches the control learns they may not, in the same channel as every other refusal this operation can produce.

**There is no emulation arm, and the absence is deliberate.** §Capability delivery's preference order ends in a typed refusal for exactly this shape of gap. Prompt-injecting an instruction to summarize the conversation would produce a plausible-looking assistant turn and move the provider's own context window not at all — spend incurred, meter unchanged, success reported. A driver that cannot compact declares `context_compaction: false` and refuses as `driver.capability_unsupported`, which the participant can see and act on.

**The Claude leg is the first V1 producer of a `driver_command` frame**, and §Required Behavior re-derives the standing negative claim about that arm rather than restating it. Because that arm is exempt from the neutralization tripwire, this section supplies the guard that stands in the tripwire's place: the command's literal name is checked against the enumeration §The provider command and skill surface reads, for that session's own binding, **before** dispatch — so a provider release that retires `/compact` refuses at the check rather than silently sending prose the model never sees — and the typed evidence above is required **after** it.

### The provider command and skill surface

`listProviderCommands` reads the provider's own enumeration of native slash-commands and skills for the bound session and returns it as observed. Both pinned providers publish this over the transport the driver already establishes, which is what makes it a read of the session rather than a walk of the operator's home directory: enumerating by filesystem would see definitions the running provider has not loaded, miss the ones it loaded from elsewhere, and require the driver to model each vendor's discovery precedence — three ways to be wrong about a set the provider will happily state.

**It is a live read, never a stored registry, and this is a design decision rather than an omission.** Nothing is persisted, nothing is cached across sessions, and no projection folds it. A stored copy would immediately need an invalidation protocol, a staleness policy, and a reconciliation path for a definition edited outside this product between two runs — machinery whose only purpose would be to re-derive what one read already gives. The direct consequence is that this capability **adds no table and no column**, and the local SQLite and Postgres censuses do not move. Where a provider announces that its own set has changed, the driver re-reads rather than patching its held copy, so the held state is always a whole reading rather than an accumulation of edits.

**The routing invariant.** Every entry carries the `(driverName, providerAccountId)` binding it was read under, and that binding governs both directions of use: an entry is **offerable** only to agents of that binding, and the one entry V1 sends — the compaction command, per the enumeration-not-dispatch rule below — is **dispatchable** only through them. A command enumerated from a Claude session is never offered to a Codex agent and never sent through one. The invariant is not defensive tidiness — the two providers share a `/name` surface syntax and share no name space behind it, so a cross-provider dispatch is either a different command or no command, and both outcomes are worse than a refusal. A dispatch that would cross the binding refuses as `driver.capability_unsupported` rather than being attempted. The account half of the key is load-bearing on its own: two accounts of the same provider can carry different skill sets, so binding to the driver alone would offer one account's definitions to another's session. An agent may hold **several live bindings at once** — V1 admits concurrent runs and a switch applied at a run boundary leaves older runs on the binding they spawned under — so the read returns one enumeration **per live binding**, each carrying the run and the `(driverName, providerAccountId)` it was read under. Collapsing them into one list is prohibited for the same reason the invariant exists at all: a flattened list is a list whose entries have lost the binding that says where they may be used.

**A disabled entry is reported, not withheld (2026-08-29).** Where the provider publishes an enabled/disabled distinction — the Codex skills surface does, the Claude handshake enumeration does not — the driver carries that declaration through **verbatim** and filters nothing: an enumeration with disabled entries silently dropped stops being the provider's own enumeration as observed, and leaves a consumer unable to tell a disabled command from one that does not exist. The flag governs **offerability** rather than presence: an entry declared disabled is not offerable and is surfaced as unavailable rather than advertised as runnable, because the bound provider will not execute it. Where the provider draws no such distinction the driver **synthesizes none** — absence means the surface has no notion of disabled, never a driver-assumed `true`.

The consumer that reads this flag is the composer surface — [Spec-023 §Signature Feature Composition Sketches](023-desktop-shell-and-renderer.md#signature-feature-composition-sketches) keys its command autocomplete on the **target agent's** driver and account, which is the routing invariant expressed as an interaction rather than as a second rule.

**V1 is enumeration and discovery; it is not a dispatch channel, and that is a contract consequence rather than a scoping preference.** Exactly one enumerated entry is sent in V1 — the compaction command, composed by the driver's own emulated leg and reached through its own control, `compactContext`, which checks presence against this same enumeration. Selecting any other entry surfaces what the provider offers and starts no turn. Three separate rules in this corpus each independently forbid the general case, and none of them is bypassable from the renderer. First, the obvious route is closed at the composer: [Spec-017 §Command interception — the reserved `/` prefix (C-18)](017-workflow-authoring-and-execution.md#command-interception--the-reserved--prefix-c-18) refuses **all** leading-`/` input on provider-bound composer paths, escape sequence included, so an autocomplete that inserted `/name` into the message box would produce text the send path refuses — the entry would not merely fail to dispatch, it would be unsendable. Second, the driver-side route is closed by §Required Behavior: the `driver_command` frame origin is the one origin exempt from the neutralization tripwire, and it is admitted only against **post-dispatch typed evidence** that the provider actually acted; an arbitrary provider command supplies no such evidence, so admitting one would either extend the tripwire exemption to unverifiable frames or record a zero-turn success as a completed turn — the exact failure the tripwire exists to catch. Third, the count is exact rather than approximate: §Required Behavior's re-derived "exactly one `driver_command` producer" claim holds only while compaction is the sole producer, and a general dispatch route would falsify it silently.

A dispatch route is therefore **named rather than left implicit**, and it is gated on both of the things that are missing: C-18's escape activated for provider-bound paths, and a per-command typed-evidence contract that says what counts as proof a given command ran. Either alone is insufficient — an activated escape with no evidence contract reintroduces the zero-turn hazard, and an evidence contract with no escape has no way to reach the composer. Until both land, the enumeration is a discovery surface, and describing it as one is the honest reading of what ships.

**Reading the enumeration requires session membership and nothing more.** It is a session-scoped read: any active role may enumerate, on the roster-read precedent that any active member may read a session's roster, and a caller who is not an active participant is refused the registered `session.not_found` — one byte-identical refusal for a non-member and for an unknown session, so the read discloses no session's existence. No Cedar action and no error code is minted for it.

### The output-speed axis

`output_speed` declares that the provider offers a participant-settable output-speed mode, and exactly one pinned provider does. Claude Code declares a fast-output state on its own session handshake, together with a reason when that state is not the one requested; codex-cli declares no such axis — and, **corrected 2026-08-31**, that is a statement about **declarability** rather than about its wire being speed-silent. The Codex cell is therefore `false` as a **complete declaration** rather than as a gap awaiting emulation: the participant's equivalent lever on that provider is the `model` / `effort` axis [Spec-016 §Same-Agent Provider Switch](016-multi-agent-channels-and-orchestration.md#same-agent-provider-switch) already carries on `agent.configUpdate`, and no speed control is emulated onto it, because effort and speed are different claims and a control that silently retargets one onto the other misreports what the participant chose.

**What the pinned Codex surface carries, and why the cell is still `false` (2026-08-31).** The negative this cell was first justified by — a census of the generated method root finding no speed, fast, turbo, or accel member across 185 method strings — is a sound measurement that was asked the wrong question. The same pin publishes a **participant-settable per-turn service tier** (`TurnStartParams.serviceTier`, in the default non-experimental generation, so a default connection can set it) and a **per-model tier catalog** (`Model.serviceTiers: Array<ModelServiceTier>` with a required-nullable `defaultServiceTier`, beside a deprecated `additionalSpeedTiers`), all of it **Generated schema, Verified** at [Codex wire reference §Output speed](../reference/provider-wire/codex.md#output-speed--a-method-level-negative-beside-a-service-tier-surface). A method census cannot settle a field-and-values question, and §Capability discovery says so in its own terms: absence in a censused registry bounds availability from below, not above. No driver broke that rule — the Codex driver resolves this flag `static` from its table, as it must — but this spec's justification prose did the thing the rule forbids, which is why the ground moves even though the cell does not. **The cell stays `false` on the axis's own conjuncts.** `output_speed` requires a **statically declarable level vocabulary** and a **declared-state read**, and the pinned Codex surface supplies neither: `ModelServiceTier` is three free-form strings, so which tiers exist is runtime catalog data rather than schema, this corpus's pinned declared Codex catalog carries no per-model tier list, and nothing on that wire reports a current tier back — `ThreadUsageBreakdownGroup.speed` is a usage-attribution grouping key and sets nothing. The only speed-differentiated tier value established anywhere at the pin is `ModelServiceTier { id: "priority", name: "Fast", description: "1.5x speed, increased usage" }`, and it is **Upstream source, Derived** — two upstream test fixtures read at the **floor** tag `rust-v0.141.0`, which establish that the vendor models such a tier and do **not** establish that any production catalog publishes it. A `false` resting on a Derived fixture would be as wrong as one resting on a method census; this one rests on the two conjuncts. **Detection stays `static` with decisiveness as the failing conjunct, on a better reason.** A zero-turn channel bearing on this axis does exist — `model/list`, measured zero-turn on 2026-08-30 — so the reason is not that the driver has only a method enumeration to consult. It is that reading a returned tier as an output-**speed** tier is a semantic judgment over free-form strings rather than a decidable read, so the channel exists and still cannot decide the flag in either direction. **Re-arm trigger.** A pinned declared catalog read carrying a speed-differentiated `ModelServiceTier` re-grades this cell by ordinary amendment. That amendment owes two decisions this section deliberately does not pre-empt: whether `outputSpeedLevels` stays statically declared or becomes per-model catalog-carried like `effortLevels`, since the static rule's justification is that reading costs a turn-bearing request and that does not hold on a zero-turn catalog read; and which boundary the axis resolves to there, since `serviceTier` is a **per-turn** override while the mechanism this section binds below is spawn-bound.

**The declared state is the truth; the request is not.** The mode sits behind two gates on the pinned provider — a settings opt-in the driver can supply, and an account-scoped gate it cannot — and the provider reports which one is refusing. So the driver reports the provider's declared state and the provider's own reason, never the value it asked for: a participant who turns the mode on and is denied by their plan sees that they were denied, not a toggle that appears to have worked. The axis rides the existing `agent.configUpdate` boundary machinery rather than minting its own operation, and a dispatch against a driver declaring `output_speed: false` refuses as `driver.capability_unsupported`.

**The value set is published, not implied.** A flag that says an axis exists does not say what may be put in it, and this contract deliberately names no speed values of its own — the vocabulary is the provider's. So a driver declaring `output_speed` also publishes `outputSpeedLevels` on the same capability read (§Provider Parameter Vocabularies), the `effortLevels` shape, and [Spec-016 §The mutation surface](016-multi-agent-channels-and-orchestration.md#the-mutation-surface) validates against that list. The declaration is **static like the flag it accompanies** — from the driver's per-driver table, never obtained by reading the provider, since reading is exactly the turn-bearing act that makes this flag `static` in the first place — and it is **served to clients**, unlike the driver-side-only `detectionSource`, because the control that offers the choice lives in the renderer. Where the list is absent or empty the axis is unsettable and the mutation refuses fail-closed; there is no arm in which an unvalidated speed value reaches a provider.

**The axis is spawn-bound, so it resolves to a `run` boundary.** [Spec-016 §Same-Agent Provider Switch](016-multi-agent-channels-and-orchestration.md#same-agent-provider-switch) resolves an axis's application boundary from the driver's **declared vocabulary** rather than by assumption: a per-turn override applies at a turn boundary, a spawn-bound one at a run boundary. The mechanism this spec binds the axis to is the pinned provider's **settings opt-in supplied at spawn** — the one whose effect was measured (§References) — so `outputSpeed` is spawn-bound and a mutation resolves to a **`run`** boundary, surfaced through that section's `pending` arm naming the boundary it will apply at. Stating this is not a formality: every other provider axis on that mutation except `driverName` and `providerAccountId` is per-turn, so a reader who does not find the boundary stated will assume the wrong one. The same provider also publishes an interactive command for this mode in the enumeration §The provider command and skill surface reads, which would be a turn-boundary mechanism — but **its programmatic effect is unmeasured**: the probe of 2026-08-29 established that the command's name is present, not that dispatching it over this transport moves the declared state. V1 therefore does not dispatch it, the axis stays bound to the measured mechanism, and a probe confirming the command's effect re-homes the axis to a turn boundary by ordinary amendment. Until then that command is **not** a second `driver_command` producer, and §Required Behavior's "exactly one" count is exact.

**The requested mode reaches the provider on the spawn, or it does not reach it at all.** The mechanism this axis binds to is a settings opt-in the provider reads at **process start**, so a fresh process is the only place a requested mode can be realized. `CreateSessionParams` therefore carries it and `ResumeSessionParams` re-realizes it, joining the spawn-bound class the resume path already keeps whole — a resume is a fresh spawn, so every spawn-bound surface the create path binds must be rebuilt there or the resumed leg silently sheds it, exactly as a posture-less resume would relaunch unsandboxed. Recovery does not hold the original request, so the value is reconstructed from the durable spawn record written at every spawn, as posture and output schema already are. Without both carriers an accepted switch would commit the agent's speed column, settle its terminal, and then start a provider process that was never told — every read built on that row asserting a mode the process is not running in, which is the same false success this section prohibits, merely produced one layer lower. The generic agent `config` blob is **not** that carrier and does not become one: it is opaque to the spawn path by construction, so a value placed there would be faithfully persisted and faithfully ignored.

**The declared state arrives on the first turn-bearing exchange, and is read rather than stored.** It does **not** come back on the spawn, and saying so would contradict this section's own detection-source ruling below, which is the reason this axis is not probed at all: the handshake that declares the state is emitted only as part of a turn-bearing exchange. A session's creation resolves before any such exchange can begin, and a resume is a fresh spawn with exactly the same shape, so neither establishment path can report the state — and neither may spend a synthetic turn or block waiting for one, which would buy the reading with the very cost the `static` classification exists to avoid. The driver instead records the provider's declared state, and its reason where the provider supplied one, **against the binding, when the handshake actually arrives** on the first turn-bearing exchange the participant's own work produces, and holds it for that binding's life — the held-state shape §The provider command and skill surface already uses for the enumeration. Until that moment the binding carries **no observation**, and that is a third distinct thing from the two absences below: a driver that declares no such axis, a binding that is not live, and a binding whose observation has not happened yet. Every reader is absent-until-observed and none of the three absences is ever rendered as the mode being off. It reaches clients as a **live-scoped** projection on the agent read model, beside the requested value and present only while the binding it was read on is live — its presence being the discriminator for "this was read from the provider", and its absence meaning nothing has been read yet or ever, rather than that the mode is off. Where an agent holds several live bindings at once, the projection is its **most recently established** one: that is the only binding whose spawn could have realized the requested value served beside it, so the pair compares like with like instead of setting a current request against an older leg's declaration. It is deliberately written to **no column**: the agent's speed column records the operator's accepted choice and the spawn record records what to re-realize, and an observation persisted into either would be a second and staler record of a fact the running session already holds — worse, it would let a mode that stopped being available across a restart keep reading as accepted. The declared value is carried **verbatim** rather than narrowed to the published vocabulary, because that vocabulary bounds what a caller may **request** while this is what the provider **reported**: a provider returning a state the driver's table does not list is reporting a real state under version skew, and coercing it to `off` would fabricate the very reading this member exists to prevent. So a provider that accepts the opt-in and then leaves the mode off is rendered as a requested value beside a differing declared one with the provider's own reason attached. That disagreement is **not a switch failure** and is not reported as one — the switch applied and the provider then declared something else — so [Spec-016 §The mutation surface](016-multi-agent-channels-and-orchestration.md#the-mutation-surface)'s `output_speed_unavailable` keeps its narrower meaning: the mode could not be **requested** at all.

**Detection source is `static`, and the reason is worth stating because it looks like it should be `probed`.** §Capability discovery admits a probe only where it is zero-turn, non-mutating, and decisive at consumption granularity. Reading this provider's declared speed state is non-mutating and fully decisive — the state is right there in the handshake — but the handshake is emitted only as part of a turn-bearing exchange, so obtaining it costs a request. **Zero-turn is the failing conjunct**, and a conjunctive test fails on one. The honest shape is a static declaration from the driver's per-driver table, paired with a runtime read of the declared state on the session the driver was going to establish anyway.

## Tool Metadata

Driver capabilities describe what the driver supports as a whole (e.g., `resume`, `steer`). Driver **tool metadata** is per-tool information the driver exposes for each tool in its catalog. The canonical V1 per-tool metadata field is `idempotency_class`, used by the daemon's two-phase command-receipt protocol during crash recovery.

### `idempotency_class`

| Value | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| `idempotent` | Safe to re-execute on recovery. Either a pure read (`file.read`, `shell.stat`) or a write whose external target is server-side idempotent (for example `S3 PutObject` with `If-Match`). |
| `compensable` | Re-executable only when paired with a caller-supplied `dedupe_key` that the remote side honors (for example [Stripe idempotency keys](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests)). The driver is responsible for propagating `dedupe_key` to the remote invocation so that a duplicate request is rejected or treated as a no-op. |
| `manual_reconcile_only` | **Not** safe to re-execute automatically. Recovery halts the run with a `recovery-needed` condition (see §Fallback Behavior) and requires operator reconciliation. Two deterministic carve-outs — persisted MCP task-handle resolution and provably-never-dispatched auto-retry — are defined in the MCP floor paragraph below; every other receipt in this class halts. |

If a driver does not declare `idempotency_class` for a tool, the runtime MUST treat it as `manual_reconcile_only`. This is the conservative default and matches the existing rule that the runtime treats undeclared capabilities as unsupported.

An MCP-discovered tool is **always** `manual_reconcile_only`. The runtime MUST NOT derive `idempotency_class` from MCP `ToolAnnotations`: the MCP specification's current revision binds clients to treat tool annotations as untrusted unless they come from trusted servers ([MCP 2025-11-25 Tools](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/server/tools), MUST-strength) — and the MUST binds trust classification, not behavior derivation, so classes bind on the operator-managed assignment surface, never on annotation self-claims. The only upgrade path is the operator-governed assignment (MCP governance, [ADR-015](../decisions/015-v1-feature-scope-definition.md) feature #18 — governed by [Spec-028](./028-mcp-server-configuration-and-governance.md) §Tool-Level Overrides, landed 2026-07-22 via the campaign's B18 bundle: Cedar-gated, trusted-server-only, always audited). The consequence is stated outright: a `manual_reconcile_only` tool in flight at daemon crash halts the run's recovery with a `recovery-needed` condition, and the halted run blocks its co-participants' dependent work in that session until an operator reconciles. Two deterministic carve-outs (Part-B fail-closed follow-up, 2026-07-17) refine that recovery dispatch without weakening the floor — the trust rule is untouched, and a class still never derives from annotations. **(a) Durable task handle**: where the call was dispatched task-augmented under the experimental [MCP 2025-11-25 Tasks](https://modelcontextprotocol.io/specification/2025-11-25/basic/utilities/tasks) utility and the receipt durably recorded the receiver-generated `taskId` from the task acceptance response (`CreateTaskResult` — the id exists only once the receiver accepts, so a crash before that response is durably stored leaves no handle and stays on the sent-but-unacked halt below), recovery resolves the receipt deterministically instead of halting: poll `tasks/get` to a terminal status (`completed | failed | cancelled`), then read the recorded outcome via `tasks/result` — the Tasks spec binds `tasks/result` to return exactly what the underlying request would have returned, so this is result retrieval, never re-execution; a task observed in `input_required` breaks the deterministic-resolution premise (it awaits interaction the recovery sweep cannot supply) and falls back to the floor's halt with the handle preserved for operator resolution, and a purged or expired task (receivers MAY delete after `ttl`) falls back the same way; retry-on-transient-poll-failure stays out of V1 while the utility is experimental upstream. **(b) Provably never dispatched**: a receipt whose send demonstrably never reached the remote side — connection refused, DNS resolution failure, or a recorded pre-send abort — is safe to auto-retry regardless of class: nothing executed, so there is nothing to reconcile. The sent-but-unacked window stays on the floor's halt — ambiguity is operator-owned. Recovery-side dispatch mirrors in [Spec-015 § Idempotency Protocol](015-persistence-recovery-and-replay.md#idempotency-protocol).

### Recovery Consequences

Recovery behavior for a receipt that was in-flight at daemon restart (Phase 2 started, Phase 3 not reached) is dispatched on `idempotency_class`. The runtime emits `tool.replayed` for an `idempotent` or `compensable` re-execution and `tool.skipped_during_recovery` for a halt; full semantics live in [Spec-015 § Idempotency Protocol](015-persistence-recovery-and-replay.md#idempotency-protocol), and event-type registration lives in [Spec-006](006-session-event-taxonomy-and-audit-log.md) (full taxonomy-row integration tracked in [BL-064](../archive/backlog-archive.md)).

## Example Flows

- `Example: A local Codex driver starts a session through its native transport, exposes resume and steer capability, and emits normalized run events into the daemon.`
- `Example: A local Claude driver calls a remote provider API from the participant's runtime node. The provider service is remote, but the canonical driver authority and policy enforcement remain local.`
- `Example: A user pauses a run. No driver supports native pause. The orchestration layer implements pause as: interrupt the active run, persist conversation history and run state to local SQLite, queue a resume. When the user resumes, a new turn is started with the full saved context. The driver only sees interruptRun followed later by startRun — it never needs to know about pause.`
- `Example: A user steers a Codex run. The orchestration layer calls applyIntervention(type: "steer", payload). The Codex driver supports steer natively via turn/steer and applies it. For a Claude run, applyIntervention returns degraded, and the orchestration layer falls back to queuing the steer content and interrupting the current turn.`
- `Example: A client retries a steer whose response was lost in transit. The retry carries the same clientIdempotencyKey; the daemon finds the persisted intervention row and returns the originally recorded outcome without dispatching the driver a second time. A different steer accidentally reusing that key is rejected as intervention.idempotency_conflict.`

## Implementation Notes

- Keep the contract small but explicit. The runtime should not need provider-name branches to answer common lifecycle questions.
- Contract versioning should allow additive capability expansion without breaking older drivers.
- Resume handle persistence belongs to the runtime binding layer, not the user-facing domain model.

## Pitfalls To Avoid

- Do not treat a version number as a capability answer. A version is admissible for one decision — whether the build is above the floor — and for nothing else. Deciding a feature from a version comparison is how a deferral outlives its own gate: the `mcp_set_servers` reconcile was deferred on a floor that had already fallen below the admission floor, and a probe would have said so on the first spawn.
- Do not read a documented exclusivity constraint as an enforced one. The Codex `permissions` / `sandboxPolicy` pair is documented mutually exclusive and measured **unenforced** at request validation, so sending both yields unspecified precedence rather than an error. Where the wire will not refuse a contradiction, the daemon must.
- Letting provider-native ids replace canonical ids
- Treating missing capability declarations as implicitly supported
- Making the session engine understand transport-specific details such as JSON-RPC framing or stdio protocol
- Deriving quota or rate-limit refusals by matching provider message text rather than the provider's typed wire signals — `reauth-required` in the closed `RecoveryCondition` set is already detected mid-run from the provider's typed auth-failure signals, and the pinned Codex surface carries rate limits as first-class typed data (`account/rateLimits/read` pull, `account/rateLimits/updated` push), so text-matching is a deliberate downgrade that breaks on provider wording changes and on localization
- Neutralizing command-shaped text with invisible or zero-width characters (`U+200B` zero-width space, `U+2060` word joiner, variation selectors, Unicode tag characters). That encoding is a live prompt-injection and data-exfiltration channel, so hardened pipelines strip or reject it — a neutralizer built on it fails open precisely where a hardened pipeline is present — and it alters the bytes the model reads while staying invisible to the human reviewing the diff. The sentinel must be a character a reviewer can see.
- Applying the neutralization anywhere upstream of the driver's outbound frame — at the client composer, at queue admission, or at event append. Text mutated before it is persisted is text the audit log no longer matches and a rewind no longer reproduces, and a second application would visibly double the sentinel; the transform belongs at the one boundary that is both after persistence and before the provider.

- Re-minting tool-call identifiers during a replay, or reusing one identifier for two distinct calls. The target's injection surface validates no pairing, so the mismatch is accepted silently and surfaces later as a conversation whose tool history contradicts the audit log.
- Stripping reasoning after repairing pairing integrity rather than before. The strip breaks pairs the repair has already fixed, and the resulting transcript is accepted without complaint.
- Treating a replay call's success return as evidence that the replay worked. The injection surfaces are untyped and fail open; only the post-replay assertion is evidence.
- Caching a rendered transcript across turns. The canonical transcript is a projection of a log that has moved; a cached render is a stale answer to a question whose source changed.
- Reading the provider's method list as its capability list. A method can be present, callable, and deprecated at the same pin, and a flag string can outlive the feature it named.

- Summing per-turn usage readings into a session total, or emitting a provider's cumulative counter as though it described a single turn. Both produce the same compounding overstatement, and the second is the easier mistake to make because the number looks entirely plausible on any one turn.
- Re-basing the usage delta at a context compaction, or differencing a late-arriving usage frame against whichever reading arrived most recently. The first forgives every token spent before the boundary; the second floors a real charge to zero and files the loss as a diagnostic. Neither is visible in the emitted row, which is why both are refused at the boundary rather than detected downstream.
- Copying a base snapshot at turn dispatch so each turn differences against its own opening reading. Two interleaved turns then each re-count the interval the other metered — the double-count is invisible per row and additive across rows. One stream-ordered base per thread and axis, with attribution by the frame's named turn, is the only shape that survives interleaving.
- Projecting a frame whose thread identity the driver could not read, or inferring the parent from arrival order. A multiplexed connection offers no positional guarantee, and the failure writes another thread's words into the participant's transcript under the parent's name. The inverse over-rotation is also a defect: demanding a thread identity from a censused connection- or account-scoped family quarantines every retry and rate-limit signal the corpus consumes, and suppressing a child's interactive requests with its transcript hangs the child mid-tool-call — the routing rule is family-scoped and the request carve-out is mandatory, per §Required Behavior.

- Emulating context compaction by asking the model to summarize the conversation. The turn succeeds, the summary is plausible, spend is real, and the provider's context window is exactly where it was — a success report for work that did not happen, which is the failure the typed-refusal arm exists to prevent.
- Reporting a compaction because the request was accepted. Both pinned mechanisms answer before the work is done — one with an empty ack, one with a command frame that only settles — so acceptance is evidence of delivery and of nothing else.
- Hardcoding a provider's command name into a `driver_command` frame instead of checking it against the enumeration the driver reads. A retired command name dispatches as prose the model never sees, on the one frame arm the neutralization tripwire is exempt from — the two failures compose into a silent one.
- Persisting the provider command and skill enumeration, or folding it into a projection. A stored copy needs invalidation, staleness, and reconciliation machinery whose whole purpose is to re-derive what one live read already gives, and it adds durable state to a capability that needs none.
- Offering an enumerated command outside the `(driverName, providerAccountId)` it was read from — including the narrower mistake of keying on the driver alone, which offers one account's skills inside another account's session.
- Reporting the requested output-speed value rather than the provider's declared one. The mode sits behind a gate the driver does not control, so a request is a request; a toggle that appears to have worked when the plan refused it is a lie the participant acts on.
- Truncating an oversized answer to a provider-raised ask so that it fits the wire, or building that ask's refusal reason out of the payload that overflowed. The first hands the model a wrong answer it cannot tell from a complete one; the second can overflow again and leave the ask unanswered by the very remedy meant to answer it.
- Swallowing the rejection of an answer write because the exit path reports it. The exit path reports that the process died, which is a different fact from a named ask going unanswered — a write can reject on a closed connection while the ask's caller is still waiting, and the exit record never names which ask that was.

## Acceptance Criteria

- [ ] A new driver can be integrated without changing session or run domain semantics.
- [ ] Unsupported capabilities remain unavailable to the user and cannot be invoked accidentally.
- [ ] Driver recovery failure produces explicit `provider failure` detail and a `RecoveryCondition` (`recovery-needed` or `reauth-required`) rather than silent session replacement.
- [ ] An intervention retried with the same `clientIdempotencyKey` never applies twice: the retry returns the original outcome, and key reuse with a differing payload is rejected.
- [ ] A logged-out provider is detected by the zero-turn `probeAuth` before any billed turn is spent.
- [ ] A successful resume reports the driver's normalized `sessionPosition`; an unparseable CLI version or below-floor provider install refuses attach fail-closed.
- [ ] An in-flight MCP receipt with a persisted task handle resolves via `tasks/get` polling + `tasks/result` retrieval rather than halting (a purged/expired or `input_required` task falls back to the halt); a receipt provably never dispatched auto-retries; every other `manual_reconcile_only` receipt keeps the operator halt.
- [ ] Participant text whose first word is command-shaped reaches the model as prose on every provider-bound path, and a provider that dispatches such a frame as a command instead fails the turn visibly rather than recording a zero-turn success. Visibly means three things on both provider-bound paths: the run halts on its `run.failed` run-lifecycle event ([Spec-006 §Run Lifecycle](006-session-event-taxonomy-and-audit-log.md#run-lifecycle-run_lifecycle)) carrying the registered cause beside a `recovery-needed` condition — the asynchronous outcome a queued or orchestration-started run surfaces through, no caller being present to answer; the run's provider binding is disposed, so no later run re-binds to a session whose client-side state may have been mutated; and an intervention caller additionally receives the cause on its own response via `DriverInterventionResult.refusalCode` whenever the trip is classified before that call resolves — a **best-effort** leg by construction, which is why the two preceding clauses, not this one, are what the criterion is met by.
- [ ] A conversation reconstituted into a fresh provider session carries every participant-visible fact the timeline shows for it, with stable tool-call identity and no unpaired tool call, and the operation reports `applied` only after the post-replay assertion passes.
- [ ] A driver declaring `transcript_replay: false`, a target that refuses the frames, and a transcript exceeding the target's context window all settle on the memo projection, report `degraded`, and carry a non-empty declared-loss list — none of the three reports `applied`.
- [ ] Provider-private reasoning appears in no exported transcript and is named on the declared-loss list of every operation that drops it, while visible reasoning summaries carry forward as plain text.
- [ ] Every capability a client can invoke is one `getCapabilities` declares; no client-reachable path is gated on the presence of a provider method alone.
- [ ] A provider build above the pin attaches and runs: every capability that still answers is kept, and only a capability whose probe refuses is withdrawn — no version above the floor refuses a session.
- [ ] Capability probes complete before the first billed turn and spend none, asserted at the provider transport: a probe run against a fresh session issues no turn-start and no user-message request, and its negative control is still refused.
- [ ] The version recorded on a run's binding is the version the spawned process reported in-band, and a driver-spawned child carries its provider's auto-update opt-out.
- [ ] The detection-mechanism table is total over each driver's flag set, every `static` entry names the admissibility conjunct that fails, and no probe mutates state the session owns — in particular no driver-side probe issues the set-replacing MCP server mutation.
- [ ] A turn is realized with exactly one member of a mutually-exclusive provider posture pair, and a turn carrying the un-realized member is refused rather than sent.
- [ ] A provider session driven across many turns reports session spend equal to its newest cumulative reading minus the establishment base, not the sum of per-turn totals: every emitted usage row is a per-axis delta from the thread's single stream-ordered base, attributed to the turn the frame names — two interleaved turns re-sum exactly, with no interval counted twice — no normalized axis counts a cached token twice and an unconfirmed containment identity diagnoses rather than silently subtracting, a fresh session's first reading meters in full (a replay-seeded session included: seeding spends nothing and the first turn's spend is real), a provider-native resume meters nothing it already emitted (the base is the prior-emitted sum, never the first post-resume reading), and a wire-declared per-turn figure that disagrees with the derived interval is diagnosed, never substituted.
- [ ] A child-thread frame never appears in the parent session's timeline and its spend never disappears: a foreign thread identity is suppressed with a diagnostic; a censused connection- or account-scoped frame routes without a thread identity while an unrecognized family without one still quarantines; a child frame racing its own parent-linked announcement is held and then released on registration, and the hold's timeout sheds with a diagnostic; an absent or unrecognized identity is quarantined under a bounded buffer rather than projected; a child's interactive request reaches the approval pipeline and is answered on the child's own correlation rather than suppressed; subagent spend lands on the subagent's own attribution, provider-internal child spend lands on the parent run, and child lifecycle is visible only as `subagent.started` / `subagent.completed`.

- [ ] `compactContext` reports success only after the provider's own typed compaction frame is observed: a mechanism that returns an accepted ack and produces no compaction boundary settles as a failure, and no path reports a compaction the driver did not witness. No code path triggers compaction from a threshold, a timer, or a heuristic — the only trigger is an explicit participant request. The wait for that evidence terminates on both of its bounds — the driver's declared per-binding bound and the binding ceasing to be live — settling `failed` in each case with a diagnostic naming which one ended it, while a compaction frame arriving after settlement is still normalized into a `usage.context_compacted` boundary, so no boundary is lost to an expired wait and no expired wait settles `applied`.
- [ ] A `driver_command` frame carrying a provider command is dispatched only after that command's literal name is found in the enumeration read for that session's own binding: with the name absent, the driver refuses rather than sending prose the provider will not act on.
- [ ] An entry returned by `listProviderCommands` is offerable only to agents of the `(driverName, providerAccountId)` it was read from, and the one entry V1 sends — the compaction command — is dispatchable only through them: a Claude-enumerated command is neither offered to nor sent through a Codex agent, and a cross-binding dispatch refuses as `driver.capability_unsupported`. Selecting any other enumerated entry inserts nothing into a composer and starts no turn, and no wire route accepts an entry as a dispatch handle. The enumeration is served from a live read: no table, column, or persisted projection holds it, and a definition changed between two sessions is picked up by the next read rather than by an invalidation path.
- [ ] The output-speed axis reports the provider's declared state and the provider's own reason when that state is not the requested one — a request the provider's account or settings gate refuses never surfaces as an enabled mode — and a driver declaring `output_speed: false` offers no speed control and emulates none onto the `model` / `effort` axis. A driver declaring `output_speed: true` publishes the axis's value set on the same capability read, that set reaches the client on both the live and the cache-reconstructed read, and a mutation carrying a value outside it — or carrying any value where the set is absent or empty — refuses fail-closed rather than forwarding the value to the provider. An accepted mode reaches the provider process: a fresh spawn and a resume both carry the requested value, and a resumed leg whose spawn record held one never relaunches at the provider's default. What the provider then declared is readable beside what was requested for as long as the binding is live, so a provider that accepts the opt-in and leaves the mode off is rendered as a disagreement with a reason rather than as an enabled mode or as a failed switch.
- [ ] Neither console-parity verb is reachable without session authorization, and neither mints a Cedar action or an error code to say so: a caller who is not an active participant is refused indistinguishably from a caller naming a session that does not exist, an active member of any role may read the command enumeration, and `compactContext` is refused for a member outside the run-control role set by the same `Action::"intervene"` adjudication the intervention path runs — the refusal arriving on the operation's own refusal arm, before the capability gate and before the driver is called.
- [ ] An answer larger than the driver's outbound frame bound reaches the provider as that ask's own refusal shape carrying a bounded constant reason, never as a truncated result or a prefix of itself, and every attempt that does not reach the provider — oversized, unencodable, or write-rejected — leaves a driver diagnostic behind rather than a silently unanswered ask.

## ADR Triggers

- If the runtime stops using a normalized driver interface, create or update `../decisions/005-provider-drivers-use-a-normalized-interface.md`.
- If a provider session ever becomes authoritative over the daemon's canonical record — or if a capability's only path becomes a provider-native verb with no daemon-side fallback — revisit [ADR-029](../decisions/029-canonical-transcript-is-authoritative.md), whose §Reversibility Assessment names that as the reversal.

## Resolved Questions and V1 Scope Decisions

- No blocking open questions remain for v1.
- V1 decision: driver capability and account-state declarations are refreshed **per runtime node on a bounded periodic cadence — 15 minutes in V1** — and may also be updated live where the provider pushes (e.g. Codex `account/rateLimits/updated` push alongside the `account/rateLimits/read` pull; the zero-turn `probeAuth` rides the same refresh). Correctness must not depend on push-only updates.
- V1 decision: the first implementation supports local-runtime-node drivers only. Shared hosted execution drivers are out of scope, even when a local driver talks to a remote provider API.
